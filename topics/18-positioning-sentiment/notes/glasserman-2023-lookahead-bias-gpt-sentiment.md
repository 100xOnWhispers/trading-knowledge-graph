## Key insight
Backtesting an LLM sentiment signal inside the model's training window is contaminated in two ways: look-ahead bias (the model may know what happened after the news) and a distraction effect (general knowledge about the named entity interferes with reading the text). Replacing entity identifiers with placeholders removes both. Surprisingly, anonymized headlines performed better in-sample, so distraction hurt more than look-ahead helped. The practical rule: test LLM signals only after the training cut-off, or anonymize.

## Method
GPT-3.5 scores news headlines as good, bad or neutral for the company, following the Lopez-Lira and Tang prompt approach. Two versions of each headline are scored: the original, and a "replaced" version in which company names, tickers and, where needed, product names are swapped for random strings. Long-only, short-only and long-short equal-weighted portfolios are formed daily from each set of scores. Performance of original and replaced portfolios is compared in-sample (inside the training window) and out-of-sample, by news source and by company size. The authors state the strategies are diagnostic tools and ignore transaction costs and short-sale constraints.

## Data and sample
In-sample: January 2015 to September 2021 (GPT-3.5 training data end). Out-of-sample: October 2021 to December 2022. Two headline sets: 129,431 web-scraped headlines covering 6,723 companies, and 181,908 Thomson Reuters headlines covering 678 S&P 500 companies. Anonymization altered 100,401 of the scraped and 180,237 of the Thomson Reuters headlines.

## Reported results
- Out-of-sample, trading on GPT-3.5 scores of original headlines substantially outperforms the market, in line with Lopez-Lira and Tang, though with lower long-short returns under the simplified strategy.
- In-sample, anonymized headlines produce higher returns than originals in both news sources. Since look-ahead bias can only help, the net negative means the distraction effect dominates.
- The effect is strongest for larger companies, about which the model knows more.
- The model shows a form of overconfidence: losses when prior knowledge misleads it exceed the gains forgone when prior knowledge would have helped.
- Out-of-sample the gap between original and anonymized loses statistical significance, but for larger companies there is borderline-significant evidence that anonymized headlines still outperform.

## Signal construction
Not a signal. It is a test protocol for any LLM-scored text feature:
1. Record the training cut-off of every model used and evaluate only on later data, or
2. Anonymize the asset name and ticker (and product names) before scoring, and
3. Report original-versus-anonymized performance to quantify contamination.

## Relevance to SOL perpetuals at minutes to hours
Highly relevant to method, not to alpha. An LLM knows that SOL rose strongly in late 2023 and fell in 2022, so a backtest of LLM-scored SOL news over those periods is suspect unless the token name is masked or the model pre-dates the period. Crypto has few assets and heavily documented price histories, so the distraction problem should be worse than for a cross-section of thousands of stocks. Masking "Solana/SOL" as a placeholder token in live scoring is a cheap experiment.

## Caveats
GPT-3.5 only; equities only; no costs. Anonymizing a crypto headline may strip information that matters (a hack of a specific chain is news because of which chain it is). The out-of-sample window is 15 months and its key comparison is not significant overall.
