# Topic 22 synthesis: deep learning on order book and high-frequency data

Thirteen source notes, all read in full text: three foundations (FI-2010, DeepLOB, the universal model), one multi-horizon extension, three critiques and benchmark studies (Lucchese, Prata, Briola), one recent transformer paper with perpetual futures data (TLOB), four crypto-specific studies (Fang, Jha, Bilokon, Wang) and one crypto failure post-mortem (Chen). Web search was unavailable for most of the session, so sources were located from known arXiv identifiers, reference lists and arXiv monthly listings; coverage of 2026 work is thin (see the last section).

## What the literature agrees on

1. **The order book predicts the next few price moves, and this is statistically solid.** A pooled LSTM beats linear models by about 10% accuracy on next-move direction across roughly 500 Nasdaq stocks, out of sample by more than a year ([Sirignano 2019](notes/sirignano-2019-universal-features-price-formation.md)). A formal model-confidence-set test rejects "no predictability" for every stock tested ([Lucchese 2022](notes/lucchese-2022-short-term-predictability-order-book.md)).
2. **The predictability is very short-lived.** It lasts 50 to 300 book updates on Nasdaq, a few milliseconds to about half a second (Lucchese). On the Binance BTC perpetual, F1 falls from about 75 at roughly one second to about 41 at roughly ten seconds for every model ([Berti 2025](notes/berti-2025-tlob-dual-attention-transformer.md)). Out-of-sample R2 for price differences on Binance peaks near 11.5% at 5 to 15 ticks ([Bilokon 2023](notes/bilokon-2023-transformers-versus-lstm-electronic-trading.md)). The neighbouring abstract-only note on [Kolm 2023](../07-order-book-imbalance/notes/kolm-2023-deep-order-flow-imbalance.md) puts the effective horizon at about two average price changes.
3. **Input representation matters more than architecture.** Models fed raw price and size levels are in the set of best models 5% to 11% of the time; the same network fed multi-level order flow or price-grid volumes is there 65% to 89% of the time (Lucchese). A plain MLP matches specialised networks (Berti); an LSTM beats time-series transformers on price differences and movement (Bilokon); logistic regression and boosted trees match DeepLOB on Bybit snapshots ([Wang 2025](notes/wang-2025-crypto-lob-better-inputs-simpler-models.md), weak evidence).
4. **Pooling instruments helps.** Universal models beat instrument-specific ones and transfer to unseen instruments in US equities (Sirignano), UK equities ([Zhang 2019](notes/zhang-2019-deeplob-cnn-lstm-limit-order-books.md), [Zhang 2021](notes/zhang-2021-multi-horizon-forecasting-lob.md)), a second Nasdaq sample (Lucchese) and Coinbase currency pairs ([Fang 2020](notes/fang-2020-price-formation-crypto-deep-learning.md)).
5. **About ten levels is enough; top of book alone is not.** L2 beats L1 clearly and L3 adds nothing for single-instrument models (Lucchese). On Coinbase BTC nothing is gained past ten levels ([Jha 2020](notes/jha-2020-deep-learning-digital-asset-lob.md)). Deeper levels matter less than the touch but are not zero (Sirignano).
6. **Benchmark scores do not generalise.** Fifteen models re-implemented and moved from FI-2010 to 2021 and 2022 Nasdaq data all fall to 48% to 61% F1, and the ranking changes ([Prata 2023](notes/prata-2023-lob-deep-learning-benchmark-lobcast.md)). FI-2010 itself is ten days of five Finnish stocks from 2010, pre-normalised, with one label threshold ([Ntakaris 2018](notes/ntakaris-2018-fi2010-benchmark-lob-dataset.md)).
7. **Microstructure decides whether the model works at all.** DeepLOB's Matthews correlation is 0.29 to 0.36 on large-tick stocks and 0.01 to 0.11 on small-tick stocks ([Briola 2024](notes/briola-2024-deep-lob-forecasting-microstructural-guide.md)). This matches the tick-size result in topic 07.
8. **Nobody shows profit after realistic costs.** Every simulation read here fills at mid-price with zero fees (Zhang 2019, Prata) or a token 0.2 basis points (Bilokon). When the label threshold is set to the spread, F1 drops to 31% to 41% (Berti). Briola argues that such backtests cannot be made reliable and replaces them with a transaction-correctness probability, which comes out at 0.06 to 0.12 for small-tick stocks. A deep-plus-evolutionary system on Binance perps with about 51% directional accuracy lost over 70% of capital against a breakeven near 55% ([Chen 2025](notes/chen-2025-red-queens-trap-deep-evolution-hft.md), grade D).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Is the learned relation stable in time?** Sirignano reports stable accuracy 18 months after training and gains from longer training histories. Fang reports live accuracy decaying to chance without frequent retraining; Prata reports lower scores in a more volatile fortnight; Berti reports lower F1 in 2015 than in 2012 for one stock; Jha sees no decay within a day but estimates regimes of hours to days. The equity evidence is far larger; the crypto evidence points the other way but rests on tiny samples.
- **Does long history help?** Sirignano: 5,000 lags (about two hours) beat 100. Zhang 2021: attention weights sit almost entirely on the latest observations. Fang: one time step is best. Wang: ten snapshots add about 2% over one. Unresolved, and it bears directly on whether a book model can say anything at minutes to hours.
- **Attention and transformers.** Prata finds the best models on both datasets use attention; Berti finds dual attention helps at longer horizons; Bilokon finds transformers worse than LSTMs on crypto and more damaged by costs.
- **Raw levels against engineered inputs.** Lucchese (and Kolm, abstract) favour order flow strongly. Prata finds a CNN-LSTM does better on raw FI-2010 levels than on the stationary features its authors proposed. The second result may be specific to pre-normalised FI-2010 data.
- **How long does crypto predictability last?** Jha says often up to a minute on Coinbase in 2019; Berti's BTC perp numbers from 2023 show most of the edge gone by ten seconds. Different years, venues, labels and class balancing.
- **Labels.** Smoothed labels that average past and future prices put known information into the target, which probably inflates accuracy (our reading; Zhang 2019 adopts them to get steadier signals, and Berti criticises the tying of the smoothing window to the horizon). Thresholds are usually tuned for class balance, not costs (Prata, Berti). Scores are not comparable across papers.
- **Thin crypto evidence.** Samples are 30 hours (Fang), 1 to 12 days (Bilokon, Berti, Wang), 9 days (Jha). None uses SOL, Hyperliquid, CME or Kalshi. Only Berti uses perpetual order book data. None tests signals from a large venue applied to a small one, and none reports results net of taker fees and latency.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. **Where does the forecast die in clock time?** Train one multi-horizon model on Binance or Bybit SOL perp books with horizons from 1 second to 30 minutes and find the last horizon at which it beats a no-change benchmark under a model confidence set. Expect seconds; the question is whether anything is left at 1 to 5 minutes.
2. **Does a deep model beat simple baselines on the same inputs?** Compare against logistic regression and boosted trees on imbalance and order-flow features (topic 23) and against linear multi-level OFI (topic 07) before investing in architecture.
3. **Order flow or volume-grid inputs against raw levels on a small-tick crypto book.** Repeat the Lucchese comparison; SOL's sparse, shifting levels should favour the robust representations.
4. **Is SOL large-tick or small-tick on each venue?** Measure spread in ticks and best-queue size on Binance, Bybit, OKX, Hyperliquid and Kalshi, then check whether predictability follows Briola's pattern.
5. **Does a pooled model transfer to Kalshi?** Train on BTC, ETH and SOL across large venues, apply to the Kalshi SOL book without retraining, and compare with a Kalshi-only model. Separately, test whether a lead-venue book model predicts Kalshi mid-price moves, which no source here examines.
6. **How fast does the model decay?** Fix a trained model and track accuracy by day for several weeks; compare with daily and intraday retraining (Fang against Sirignano).
7. **Does history beyond a few seconds add anything?** Compare 100-step, 1,000-step and multi-hour context windows, including aggregated flow features, at the 1 to 5 minute horizon.
8. **Does anything survive cost-based labels?** Label only moves exceeding the Kalshi round-trip cost (spread plus fees, plus funding where relevant) and report precision on those labels and p_T, not F1 on balanced classes.
9. **Is the short-horizon forecast useful as a timing overlay?** Given a direction decided on other grounds, test whether entering when the book model agrees improves average entry price net of the delay.
