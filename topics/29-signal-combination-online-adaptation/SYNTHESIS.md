# Combining weak signals and adapting online: synthesis

Scope: the layer between individual signals and the position. It covers forecast combination under instability, alpha combination and allocation across strategies, prediction with expert advice and bandits (the reinforcement-learning-flavoured answer to "which strategy now?"), concept-drift detection and live monitoring, and the evidence on whether learned combiners (stacking, meta-learning, dynamic re-selection, bandit selectors) beat equal weighting out of sample after costs. Single-model prediction (topics 22, 23), full reinforcement-learning agents (24), search over rule structure (25) and backtest statistics (26) are excluded and cross-linked where they touch this layer.

Sixteen notes: thirteen from full text, two abstract-only (Rapach, Strauss and Zhou 2010; DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal 2009), one secondary (Lopez de Prado 2016 on hierarchical risk parity, via an encyclopedia article). Web search was unavailable for most of the session, so sources were limited to those reachable by direct address; the gaps are listed at the end of the next section.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Combine; do not select.** Averaging forecasts beats picking the model with the best track record in almost every comparison surveyed ([Timmermann 2006](notes/timmermann-2006-forecast-combinations-handbook.md); [Wang et al. 2022](notes/wang-2022-forecast-combinations-fifty-year-review.md)). In finance, combining univariate predictive regressions rescued out-of-sample equity premium prediction where each regression alone failed ([Rapach et al. 2010](notes/rapach-2010-equity-premium-combination-forecasts.md), abstract only). In crypto, dynamic model averaging beat dynamic model selection in nearly every cell and kitchen-sink regressions were erratic, up to 12 times the benchmark error ([Catania et al. 2018](notes/catania-2018-forecasting-cryptocurrencies-dynamic-model-averaging.md)). Theory agrees: a deterministic "follow one expert" rule can be forced to make twice the best expert's mistakes, while randomized or blended exponential weighting cannot ([Hazan 2023](notes/hazan-2023-introduction-online-convex-optimization.md)).

2. **Equal weighting is the benchmark to beat, and the reason is estimation error.** The "forecast combination puzzle" (simple averages match or beat estimated optimal weights) is explained by weight-estimation error when optimal weights are near equal, samples are short and the environment shifts ([Timmermann 2006](notes/timmermann-2006-forecast-combinations-handbook.md); [Wang et al. 2022](notes/wang-2022-forecast-combinations-fifty-year-review.md)). The portfolio version: none of 14 optimizing models consistently beat 1/N across seven datasets, and mean-variance would need about 3,000 months of data for 25 assets ([DeMiguel et al. 2009](notes/demiguel-2009-optimal-versus-naive-diversification.md), abstract only).

3. **If weights are estimated, ignore or regularize correlations, shrink hard toward equal, and trim the worst.** Inverse-MSE weights that ignore correlations, heavy shrinkage toward equal weights, and trimming before combining are the modifications with the best record ([Timmermann 2006](notes/timmermann-2006-forecast-combinations-handbook.md)). The practitioner derivation for very many signals reaches a compatible end point: with more signals than observations, Sharpe-optimal weights reduce to a regression residual scaled by inverse variance, and the fallback with no information is inverse variance ([Kakushadze and Yu 2016](notes/kakushadze-2016-how-to-combine-billion-alphas.md)).

4. **Second moments are learnable on trading timescales; first moments mostly are not.** This is why variance-only allocators (inverse variance, hierarchical risk parity) are defensible where mean-variance is not ([DeMiguel et al. 2009](notes/demiguel-2009-optimal-versus-naive-diversification.md); [Lopez de Prado 2016](notes/lopezdeprado-2016-hierarchical-risk-parity-diversified-portfolios.md), secondary). It matches the minimum-backtest-length argument in topic 26 ([Bailey et al. 2014](../26-backtest-overfitting/notes/bailey-2014-pseudo-mathematics-financial-charlatanism.md)).

5. **Adaptation has a price that can be written down.** With abrupt breaks, no bandit policy has regret below order sqrt(T), and discounted or sliding-window UCB reach that up to logarithms ([Garivier and Moulines 2008](notes/garivier-2008-ucb-policies-non-stationary-bandits.md)). With continuous drift bounded by a budget V, minimax regret is of order V^(1/3) T^(2/3), and with drift proportional to time no policy tracks the best arm ([Besbes et al. 2019](notes/besbes-2019-bandit-non-stationary-rewards-variation-budget.md)). A rule tuned for a stationary world (logarithmic regret) cannot also adapt: standard UCB-1 and strongly convex online gradient descent both lock on ([Garivier and Moulines 2008](notes/garivier-2008-ucb-policies-non-stationary-bandits.md); [Hazan 2023](notes/hazan-2023-introduction-online-convex-optimization.md)).

6. **Forgetting must be explicit, and smooth or abrupt forgetting are both acceptable.** Forgetting factors in dynamic model averaging ([Catania et al. 2018](notes/catania-2018-forecasting-cryptocurrencies-dynamic-model-averaging.md)), fixed-share mixing in expert weighting ([Hazan 2023](notes/hazan-2023-introduction-online-convex-optimization.md); [Li and Hoi 2013](notes/li-2013-online-portfolio-selection-survey.md)), discounting or windows in bandits, periodic restarts ([Besbes et al. 2019](notes/besbes-2019-bandit-non-stationary-rewards-variation-budget.md)) and fading factors in stream learning ([Gama et al. 2014](notes/gama-2014-survey-concept-drift-adaptation.md)) are the same idea. The memory length should follow the expected time between changes; ADWIN chooses it from the data ([Bifet and Gavalda 2007](notes/bifet-2007-adwin-adaptive-windowing.md)).

7. **Detectors trade delay against false alarms and cannot escape the trade.** CUSUM, Page-Hinkley, control-chart and two-window detectors all expose one or two parameters that set this balance ([Gama et al. 2014](notes/gama-2014-survey-concept-drift-adaptation.md)). ADWIN's own bound shows the detectable shift shrinks only with the square root of the sample ([Bifet and Gavalda 2007](notes/bifet-2007-adwin-adaptive-windowing.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Do time-varying weights help?** Timmermann calls the evidence "mixed" and quotes Stock and Watson finding that more time variation makes combinations less robust. Against that, dynamic model averaging gave significant gains for two of four coins ([Catania et al. 2018](notes/catania-2018-forecasting-cryptocurrencies-dynamic-model-averaging.md)) and daily re-selection raised the information coefficient relative to fixed fitted weights in Chinese equities, from 2.43% to 4.40% on one index and from 2.05% to 2.84% on another ([Shi et al. 2024](notes/shi-2024-alphaforge-dynamic-alpha-combination.md)). Neither of the two positive studies includes an equal-weight combination, so they show that adaptive beats static-fitted, not that adaptive beats 1/N.
- **Do learned combiners beat equal weights?** Where the question was actually asked with 1/N in the comparison, the answer is weak. A Thompson-sampling strategy selector improved Sharpe slightly on monthly data and was level or below equal weights on daily data ([Zhu et al. 2019](notes/zhu-2019-portfolio-bandit-thompson-sampling-strategies.md)). A non-stationary bandit network reports Sharpe 1.59 against 1.32 for equal weights, but on 44 hand-picked stocks, one regime, no costs, and with one of its own configurations below equal weights ([Fonseca et al. 2024](notes/fonseca-2024-bandit-networks-portfolio-optimization.md)). Feature-based meta-learning did win at scale (second place in the M4 competition), but with 100,000 series to learn from ([Wang et al. 2022](notes/wang-2022-forecast-combinations-fifty-year-review.md)). Related in-repository evidence points the same way: an expert-switching selector gained little in crypto ([Asadi 2026](../01-macro-regime-detection/notes/asadi-2026-regime-aware-llm-expert-switching.md)), mixtures of experts did not beat simple execution baselines ([Ardaiz 2026](../24-reinforcement-learning/notes/ardaiz-2026-mixture-experts-crypto-execution-failure-modes.md)), and a majority-vote ensemble helped by trading rarely more than by forecasting better ([Sebastiao and Godinho 2021](../23-ml-feature-prediction/notes/sebastiao-2021-forecasting-trading-crypto-machine-learning.md)).
- **Theory versus practice in online learning.** Regret bounds favour follow-the-winner methods with guarantees, yet the survey reports that heuristic mean-reversion methods without guarantees did best in the standard back-tests ([Li and Hoi 2013](notes/li-2013-online-portfolio-selection-survey.md)). In one applied paper stationary UCB-1 had a higher Sharpe than every non-stationary variant ([Fonseca et al. 2024](notes/fonseca-2024-bandit-networks-portfolio-optimization.md)), the opposite of what the synthetic experiments show ([Garivier and Moulines 2008](notes/garivier-2008-ucb-policies-non-stationary-bandits.md)).
- **Filter correlated signals or keep them?** The combination literature says trim and cluster; the alpha-mining literature shows that a signal correlated with the pool can still improve the combination ([Yu et al. 2023](../25-evolutionary-algorithms/notes/yu-2023-alphagen-synergistic-formulaic-alphas-rl.md)), and the regression-residual view pays a signal only for its unexplained part ([Kakushadze and Yu 2016](notes/kakushadze-2016-how-to-combine-billion-alphas.md)).
- **Costs are almost never in the evaluation.** See the audit table below. Not one of the sixteen sources evaluates a combiner at intraday frequency net of fees, and none uses perpetual futures. Combining signals with different decay rates under costs is answered in this repository only by [Garleanu and Pedersen 2009](../27-execution-risk-management/notes/garleanu-2009-dynamic-trading-predictable-returns-costs.md) in topic 27.
- **Crypto evidence is one daily working paper ending in 2017.** Nothing here tests combination, bandit selection or drift detection on SOL or on any minute-to-hour crypto series.
- **Drift detection has no trading evidence in what was opened.** The detectors come with guarantees for bounded independent data; none of the sources measures detection delay for a strategy losing a small edge under heavy-tailed, autocorrelated P&L. Topic 26's tools (probabilistic Sharpe ratio and minimum track record length, [Bailey and Lopez de Prado 2012](../26-backtest-overfitting/notes/bailey-2012-sharpe-ratio-efficient-frontier.md); drawdown and time-under-water limits, [Bailey and Lopez de Prado 2014](../26-backtest-overfitting/notes/bailey-2014-stop-outs-serial-correlation-triple-penance.md)) remain the only finance-specific monitoring rules in the repository, with Bayesian online change-point detection ([Adams and MacKay 2007](../01-macro-regime-detection/notes/adams-2007-bayesian-online-changepoint-detection.md)) as the model-based alternative.
- **Not covered because the source could not be reached:** choice of estimation window under breaks (Pesaran and Timmermann 2007); forecast comparison tests in unstable environments (Giacomini and Rossi 2010; only its abstract record was seen, describing a Fluctuation test and a One-Time Reversal test of relative local performance); complete subset regressions (Elliott, Gargano and Timmermann 2013); the original dynamic model averaging papers (Raftery et al. 2010; Koop and Korobilis 2012); the fundamental law of active management (Grinold and Kahn); Isichenko's book; the original CUSUM paper (Page 1954) and statistical-process-control monitoring of managers (Philips, Yashchin and Stein); meta-labeling beyond the note already in topic 23 ([Singh and Joubert 2019](../23-ml-feature-prediction/notes/singh-2019-meta-labeling-triple-barrier-signal-efficacy.md)); contextual bandits; and any evolutionary ensemble-selection study (topic 25 covers evolutionary search for the signals themselves, for example [Zhang et al. 2020](../25-evolutionary-algorithms/notes/zhang-2020-autoalpha-hierarchical-evolutionary-alpha-mining.md)). Open copies of the Thompson sampling tutorial (Russo et al.) and a later drift review (Lu et al.) exist on arXiv but were not read.

## Candidate signals and methods

These are combination, allocation and monitoring methods, not alpha signals. "Horizon" is the cadence at which the method would update in a minutes-to-hours bot.

A layered default that follows from the notes: standardize signals; trim; equal-risk weights within signal families and across families; a slow, shrunk tilt from discounted performance (exponential weights with fixed share); a detector per signal that can halve or zero its weight; and strategy-level stops from topic 26 above all of it. Each layer must be shown to beat the one before it net of costs.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. On the bot's own signal set at 5, 15 and 60 minute bars, does any estimated weighting (inverse-MSE, shrunk regression, dynamic model averaging, exponential weights) beat the equal-risk blend in net Sharpe after taker fees and funding? Report turnover for each.
2. Is there performance persistence in signal quality? Measure the rank correlation between each signal's trailing information coefficient and its next-window information coefficient for look-backs from one day to one month. All adaptive weighting depends on this being positive; if it is near zero, equal weights are optimal by the puzzle's logic.
3. How large is the drift budget? Estimate the cumulative change in each signal's rolling edge and derive the implied memory length; compare with the memory that performs best in a walk-forward test ([Mroziewicz and Slepaczuk 2026](../26-backtest-overfitting/notes/mroziewicz-2026-walk-forward-window-optimization-bitcoin.md) treat window lengths as hyperparameters).
4. Detection delay under realistic P&L: inject a known loss of edge (for example, set a signal's forecast to noise from a chosen date) into historical data and measure how many trades Page-Hinkley, a control chart, ADWIN, Bayesian change-point detection and the minimum-track-record rule need to flag it at a fixed false-alarm rate calibrated on the signal's in-control history.
5. Does a kill switch add value or only cut variance? Compare always-on, drawdown-stop, and detector-based switch-off with a re-entry rule, counting the edge lost to false alarms ([Bailey and Lopez de Prado 2014](../26-backtest-overfitting/notes/bailey-2014-stop-outs-serial-correlation-triple-penance.md) show normal drawdowns are longer than intuition suggests).
6. Full-information versus bandit feedback: since paper returns of all strategies are observable, does a bandit add anything over exponential weights, or is it useful only where market impact and fills make paper returns unreliable (for example on Kalshi's thin book)?
7. Does pooling many perps (Binance and Bybit majors) to train one combiner, then applying it to SOL on Kalshi, beat a SOL-only combiner? This is the cross-learning condition under which learned combiners have won elsewhere.
8. Family-then-member equal weighting versus hierarchical risk parity versus flat equal weighting across all signal variants: which is most stable when a search process adds many near-duplicate features?
9. How should weights account for decay speed? Combine the performance-based weight with the persistence-based aim portfolio of [Garleanu and Pedersen 2009](../27-execution-risk-management/notes/garleanu-2009-dynamic-trading-predictable-returns-costs.md) and test whether fast signals deserve less weight than their gross information coefficient suggests once fees are charged.

## Reading order

1. [Timmermann 2006](notes/timmermann-2006-forecast-combinations-handbook.md): why combination works and why equal weights are hard to beat.
2. [DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal 2009](notes/demiguel-2009-optimal-versus-naive-diversification.md) and [Rapach, Strauss and Zhou 2010](notes/rapach-2010-equity-premium-combination-forecasts.md): the two finance anchors (both abstract only).
3. [Wang, Hyndman, Li and Kang 2022](notes/wang-2022-forecast-combinations-fifty-year-review.md): the puzzle, and when learned combiners have worked.
4. [Catania, Grassi and Ravazzolo 2018](notes/catania-2018-forecasting-cryptocurrencies-dynamic-model-averaging.md): dynamic model averaging on crypto.
5. [Kakushadze and Yu 2016](notes/kakushadze-2016-how-to-combine-billion-alphas.md) and [Lopez de Prado 2016](notes/lopezdeprado-2016-hierarchical-risk-parity-diversified-portfolios.md): practitioner weighting when correlations cannot be estimated well.
6. [Hazan 2023](notes/hazan-2023-introduction-online-convex-optimization.md) then [Li and Hoi 2013](notes/li-2013-online-portfolio-selection-survey.md): expert advice, fixed share, and the online portfolio literature.
7. [Garivier and Moulines 2008](notes/garivier-2008-ucb-policies-non-stationary-bandits.md) and [Besbes, Gur and Zeevi 2019](notes/besbes-2019-bandit-non-stationary-rewards-variation-budget.md): what non-stationarity costs.
8. [Zhu et al. 2019](notes/zhu-2019-portfolio-bandit-thompson-sampling-strategies.md), [Fonseca et al. 2024](notes/fonseca-2024-bandit-networks-portfolio-optimization.md) and [Shi et al. 2024](notes/shi-2024-alphaforge-dynamic-alpha-combination.md): applied tests of learned selection and dynamic combination, read critically.
9. [Gama et al. 2014](notes/gama-2014-survey-concept-drift-adaptation.md) and [Bifet and Gavalda 2007](notes/bifet-2007-adwin-adaptive-windowing.md): drift detection and the monitoring layer, alongside the live-monitoring notes in topic 26.
