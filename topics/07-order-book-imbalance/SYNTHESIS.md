# Topic 07 synthesis: order book imbalance and order flow imbalance

Sixteen source notes: seven foundational equity papers, five crypto-specific studies (2019 to 2026), and four works on machine learning, reinforcement learning and evolutionary search applied to these features. Three notes rest on abstracts only (Stoikov, Kolm, Silantyev); they are marked as such.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Order flow imbalance explains contemporaneous price change, linearly, with slope inversely proportional to depth.** Best-level OFI explains about 65% of 10-second mid-price variance in US equities ([Cont 2014](notes/cont-2014-price-impact-order-book-events.md)); a ten-level integrated OFI raises in-sample explanatory power to about 87% ([Cont 2023](notes/cont-2023-cross-impact-order-flow-imbalance.md)); deeper levels help most for large-tick instruments ([Xu 2019](notes/xu-2019-multi-level-order-flow-imbalance.md)).
2. **Static top-of-book imbalance predicts the direction of the next price move.** Out-of-sample AUC of 0.7 to 0.8 for large-tick stocks and 0.6 to 0.65 for small-tick stocks ([Gould 2016](notes/gould-2016-queue-imbalance-one-tick-ahead.md)); the expected move is about linear in imbalance and bounded by a fraction of the spread ([Lipton 2013](notes/lipton-2013-trade-arrival-quote-imbalance.md)); the micro-price packages this as a fair-value adjustment ([Stoikov 2018](notes/stoikov-2018-micro-price-high-frequency-estimator.md)).
3. **Tick size governs signal strength.** Every study that splits by tick regime finds imbalance more informative when the tick is large relative to price, in equities (Gould, Xu) and in crypto perps ([Bieganowski 2026](notes/bieganowski-2026-explainable-patterns-crypto-microstructure.md)).
4. **Predictive power decays within seconds to a few minutes.** The effective horizon of book-based forecasts is about two average price changes ([Kolm 2023](notes/kolm-2023-deep-order-flow-imbalance.md), abstract); lagged cross-asset OFI helps at one minute and its advantage shrinks over horizons out to 30 minutes (Cont 2023); crypto studies work at 1 to 3 seconds (Bieganowski, [Albers 2025](notes/albers-2025-market-makers-dilemma-fill-probability.md)).
5. **The edge is smaller than the cost of crossing the spread plus fees.** Lipton says so for equities; Albers measures it live on the Binance BTC perp (about +1 bp per round trip before fees, negative after a 1.5 bp taker fee per leg).
6. **Imbalance is most valuable for execution and adverse-selection control.** Conditioning quotes on a three-state imbalance regime turns a losing maker strategy into a profitable one out of sample ([Cartea 2018](notes/cartea-2018-enhancing-trading-order-book-signals.md)).
7. **Use flow (changes) rather than raw book levels as model inputs, and regularize across levels.** Kolm (abstract), Xu (Ridge over OLS), Cont 2023 (PCA).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **OFI versus trade flow in crypto.** Equities: OFI dominates trade imbalance (65% against 32% R2, Cont 2014). BitMEX XBTUSD in 2019: trade flow imbalance explains price change better ([Silantyev 2019](notes/silantyev-2019-order-flow-analysis-crypto-markets.md), abstract only). The ranking appears to depend on depth and quote update rate, and has not been re-tested on 2024 to 2026 perps in any source read here.
- **Does conditioning passive orders on imbalance work?** Cartea reports large out-of-sample gains on 2014 Nasdaq stocks; Albers finds every simple imbalance-conditioned maker and taker strategy loses money live on a crypto perp, because fill probability and post-fill return are negatively correlated. Differences: fill assumptions (front of queue in Cartea), fees, a decade of competition, and tick regime.
- **Is the signal tradable in crypto at all?** Bieganowski reports profitable taker backtests for small-cap perps but not for BTC or LTC, without latency, and with one flash-crash day driving a large share of results. Albers, with real orders, is pessimistic for BTC.
- **Do deeper levels help forecasting?** They clearly help contemporaneous fit (Xu, Cont 2023), but Cont 2023 finds integrated OFI no better than best-level OFI for one-minute-ahead prediction. In crypto, deep levels are where non-bona fide orders sit: 31% of large Coinbase orders were flagged as potentially spoofing, almost none at the top of book ([Fabre 2025](notes/fabre-2025-spoofability-limit-order-books.md)).
- **Is order flow universally informative?** [Jeon 2026](notes/jeon-2026-order-flow-state-dependent-crypto-futures.md) finds, at 1 and 5 minutes on Binance perps, that flow adds to book state for ETH but not robustly for BTC, and mostly in stressed regimes. Bieganowski argues feature effects are universal across coins at 3 seconds. The targets differ (liquidity state against return), so the two are not strictly in conflict, but the question for SOL is open.
- **Machine learning, RL and evolutionary methods.** Evidence that these add net-of-cost value on top of the linear signals is weak in this topic. The OFI plus Q-learning pipeline of [Jaddu 2023](notes/jaddu-2023-deep-learning-rl-order-flow-imbalance.md) lost money live because the agent leaned on a horizon shorter than its latency; [Sadighian 2019](notes/sadighian-2019-drl-crypto-market-making.md) reports positive results on two test days with optimistic fills; evolved rules in [Funie 2014](notes/funie-2014-genetic-programming-swarm-hft-rules.md) were profitable on 2003 FX data and not on 2008 data. Boosted trees with engineered imbalance features (Bieganowski) are the best-supported ML approach here.
- **Coverage gaps in the evidence.** No source read here tests imbalance signals on SOL, on Hyperliquid, on Kalshi, or at horizons beyond 30 minutes. Almost nothing reports results net of realistic crypto fees and latency except Albers.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. **Horizon of predictability in price-change units.** On Binance, Bybit, OKX and Hyperliquid SOL perps, at what number of average price changes does out-of-sample R2 of OFI-based forecasts reach zero? Convert to clock time by hour of day.
2. **Cross-venue lead to Kalshi.** Does minute-level integrated OFI on the large venues predict the Kalshi SOL perp's next 1, 5, 15 and 60 minute return after controlling for the contemporaneous price gap between venues? This is the cross-impact design of Cont 2023 with venues in place of stocks.
3. **Aggregation to the bot's horizon.** Does OFI summed over 5 to 60 minutes carry information for the next 5 to 60 minutes, or is it purely a description of the move that already happened? Test with and without trade-flow controls from topic 08.
4. **OFI versus trade flow by venue.** Repeat the Silantyev comparison on each venue for SOL; expect the thin venue to be trade-driven and the deep venue quote-driven.
5. **Tick regime.** Measure SOL's relative tick and spread-in-ticks on each venue, then check whether imbalance informativeness lines up with the large-tick and small-tick pattern.
6. **Fee break-even.** Reproduce the Albers taker and maker markout tables for SOL with the bot's real fee tier and latency on each execution venue.
7. **State dependence.** Apply the Jeon layered protocol with a price-direction target: does imbalance or flow add value only in stressed liquidity states, around macro releases, or near funding timestamps?
8. **Spoof robustness.** Compare multi-level imbalance with and without filtering large, young, far-from-touch orders; check which version forecasts better and which is more stable.
9. **ML, RL and evolutionary add-ons.** Does a boosted-tree model, a small tabular RL policy trained on delayed forecasts, or a genetic-programming search over the feature set beat a regularized linear model net of costs, under walk-forward testing with the stability checks from Funie?
10. **Coarse-tick proxy.** If Kalshi's tick is coarse relative to Binance, does Kalshi book imbalance track the position of the Binance price inside the Kalshi spread, as Bieganowski found for a spot and perp pair?
