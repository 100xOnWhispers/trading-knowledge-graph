## Key insight
White's Reality Check loses power when the candidate set contains poor or irrelevant alternatives, and it can be manipulated by adding them. Hansen's test for superior predictive ability (SPA) fixes this with two changes: a studentized test statistic and a sample-dependent null distribution that discounts candidates that are clearly worse than the benchmark.

## Method
With d_k,t the performance of candidate k relative to the benchmark and d̄_k its sample mean over n periods:

T_SPA = max[ max over k of ( sqrt(n) · d̄_k / ω̂_k ), 0 ],

where ω̂_k² is a consistent estimate of the variance of sqrt(n)·d̄_k. The null distribution is generated by a stationary bootstrap around a recentring vector μ̂_c with

μ̂_c,k = d̄_k · 1{ sqrt(n)·d̄_k / ω̂_k ≤ −sqrt(2 log log n) },

so candidates significantly worse than the benchmark keep their negative mean and do not inflate the null. Two bounds are also reported: a lower one (SPA_l) and an upper, least-favourable one (SPA_u, which corresponds to the Reality Check's treatment). A wide gap between them signals that poor alternatives are present.

## Data and sample
Monte Carlo experiments with m = 100 and m = 1,000 alternatives, and an empirical exercise forecasting annual US inflation: 3,304 regression-based forecasts (all subsets of 1 to 3 from 27 regressors, plus their average) against a random-walk benchmark, sample size n = 160, 10,000 bootstrap resamples.

## Reported results
- In a two-alternative example, studentization raises local power from about 15% to about 53%.
- In simulations with poor alternatives present, the Reality Check "never rejects" in one design where the consistent SPA test has power close to 84%.
- Both modifications, studentization and the data-dependent null, give substantial power gains.
- Inflation, large universe (3,304 forecasts): SPA consistent p-value 0.741, so no evidence any forecast beats the random walk; the lower and upper p-values differ widely, indicating poor alternatives.
- Inflation, small universe (352 forecasts): SPA consistent p-value 0.048. Hansen notes that reporting the small universe after seeing the large one is not fully in the spirit of the test.

## Signal construction
Not a signal. Use it in place of the Reality Check for any search over rules or models. Inputs: the matrix of per-period net performance differentials of every candidate versus the benchmark. Report the consistent p-value together with the lower and upper bounds.

## Relevance to SOL perpetuals at minutes to hours
Automated searches (grid, Bayesian, genetic programming, random feature generation) produce universes dominated by bad candidates, exactly the setting where the Reality Check is weakest. SPA is therefore the better default for a crypto rule search. Because strategies differ greatly in volatility (a one-minute scalper against an hourly trend rule), studentization also prevents a noisy high-variance rule from setting the maximum. The loss function should be net return after fees, funding and slippage.

## Caveats
Still a test of the single best candidate against a benchmark; it does not control the false discovery rate across the set (stepwise extensions exist but were not read here). The threshold sqrt(2 log log n) is asymptotic; the simulations show a slight over-rejection (about 1%) at n = 200 that disappears at n = 1,000. Bootstrap dependence parameter matters for intraday data. As with White's test, the universe must be complete and fixed in advance.
