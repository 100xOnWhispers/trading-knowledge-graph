# Macro and market regime detection: synthesis

Sixteen source notes: thirteen read in full, three from abstracts only (marked below). Evidence at the bot's own horizon and asset is thin. Most rigorous results are daily or monthly and on equities or futures; the crypto and intraday sources are weaker. Read the grades.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Regimes are mostly volatility regimes.** On monthly equity, rate and currency returns, equal means across regimes cannot be rejected while equal volatilities are strongly rejected ([ang-2011](notes/ang-2011-regime-changes-financial-markets.md)). The crypto HMM study finds the same pattern, with states that track volatility and alternate often ([koki-2020](notes/koki-2020-predictability-cryptocurrencies-bayesian-hmm.md)). A regime label is a reliable guide to risk and a poor guide to direction.

2. **The tested payoff is risk reduction, not return prediction.** Strategies that cut exposure in the turbulent state lowered volatility by about 41% on average ([bulla-2010](notes/bulla-2010-markov-switching-asset-allocation-profitable.md)) and roughly halved maximum drawdown ([shu-2024](notes/shu-2024-downside-risk-statistical-jump-model.md)), net of 10 bp costs and out of sample. Return gains were modest: 0.2 to 2 points a year in the first study and 1 to 4 points for the jump model in the second, where the HMM version trailed buy-and-hold in two of three markets.

3. **Only filtered, frozen labels count.** The Hamilton filter gives the probability of each regime using data up to now; smoothed probabilities use the future ([hamilton-2005](notes/hamilton-2005-regime-switching-models-palgrave.md)). A valid test refits on data available at the time and never revises a label ([chauvet-2005](notes/chauvet-2005-real-time-business-cycle-dating.md), [shu-2024](notes/shu-2024-downside-risk-statistical-jump-model.md)). Even a correctly specified filter misses switches and raises false alarms ([ang-2011](notes/ang-2011-regime-changes-financial-markets.md)).

4. **Persistence has to be imposed, and it costs delay.** A raw HMM on daily returns changed state 8.5 times a year where a penalised jump model changed less than once ([shu-2024](notes/shu-2024-downside-risk-statistical-jump-model.md)). Every working implementation adds a persistence device: a median filter ([bulla-2010](notes/bulla-2010-markov-switching-asset-allocation-profitable.md)), a threshold held for several periods ([chauvet-2005](notes/chauvet-2005-real-time-business-cycle-dating.md)), a jump penalty ([shu-2024](notes/shu-2024-downside-risk-statistical-jump-model.md), [nystrup-2020](notes/nystrup-2020-greedy-online-classification-market-states.md), abstract only), or buffer zones ([agakishiev-2025](notes/agakishiev-2025-regime-switching-reinforcement-learning-crypto.md), abstract only). Each one delays recognition of a new regime.

5. **Two states are usually too few.** Four states for stocks and bonds, including a short crash state that exits into a volatile recovery state ([guidolin-2007](notes/guidolin-2007-asset-allocation-multivariate-regime-switching.md)); four for Bitcoin ([koki-2020](notes/koki-2020-predictability-cryptocurrencies-bayesian-hmm.md)); three for large cryptocurrencies ([cortese-2023](notes/cortese-2023-cryptocurrency-returns-sparse-jump-model.md), abstract only). Formal tests for the number of states are non-standard ([hamilton-2005](notes/hamilton-2005-regime-switching-models-palgrave.md)), and information criteria can pick degenerate states ([joshi-2025](notes/joshi-2025-regime-specific-interdependencies-crypto-gmm-var.md)).

6. **Gaussian HMMs are fragile on jumpy data.** On simulated jump-diffusion paths a Gaussian HMM identified 0.66% of stressed-regime observations, against 87% for distribution-based clustering ([horvath-2021](notes/horvath-2021-clustering-market-regimes-wasserstein.md)). Clustering and jump-model approaches accept arbitrary features and make no distributional assumption.

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **HMM or not.** [bulla-2010](notes/bulla-2010-markov-switching-asset-allocation-profitable.md) answers "yes" to whether profitable HMM strategies exist; [shu-2024](notes/shu-2024-downside-risk-statistical-jump-model.md), with a longer sample and tuned smoothing, finds the HMM strategy trailing buy-and-hold on return in two of three markets. The difference may be sample period, smoothing choice or tuning; no source reconciles them.
- **Hard switching or regime as a feature.** Rule-based switching ([bulla-2010](notes/bulla-2010-markov-switching-asset-allocation-profitable.md), [shu-2024](notes/shu-2024-downside-risk-statistical-jump-model.md)) is transparent and testable. Feeding a changepoint score into a learner raised a deep momentum model's Sharpe ratio by about a third ([wood-2022](notes/wood-2022-slow-momentum-fast-reversion-changepoint.md)), but that edge vanished above about 2 bp of cost.
- **Recurring regimes or one-off breaks.** Markov switching assumes states recur; changepoint models assume each segment is new ([adams-2007](notes/adams-2007-bayesian-online-changepoint-detection.md)). Crypto has both: recurring volatility states and structural changes such as new venues and products. No source compares the two families on the same data.
- **Does switching among learned agents add anything?** The 2026 expert-switching paper reports a gain in crypto of 71% against 68% cumulative return for its "best fixed" baseline, while its own table of standalone agents shows 76% and 71% ([asadi-2026](notes/asadi-2026-regime-aware-llm-expert-switching.md)). Naive recent-performance gating was worse than no switching in currencies. The one regime-switching reinforcement-learning study on hourly crypto could only be read as an abstract, which reports "potential" with unnamed caveats ([agakishiev-2025](notes/agakishiev-2025-regime-switching-reinforcement-learning-crypto.md)).
- **Regime-conditioned lead-lag in crypto is ambiguous.** At one-minute frequency, BTC's share of other coins' variance rises in volatile minutes, yet lagged predictability tests are far stronger in calm minutes ([joshi-2025](notes/joshi-2025-regime-specific-interdependencies-crypto-gmm-var.md)). The study assigns regimes with full-sample information.
- **Weak spots overall.** Nothing here tests a regime detector on SOL, on perpetuals, or with funding and open interest as inputs. The crypto sources end in 2019 ([koki-2020](notes/koki-2020-predictability-cryptocurrencies-bayesian-hmm.md)), use full-sample labels ([joshi-2025](notes/joshi-2025-regime-specific-interdependencies-crypto-gmm-var.md)), or were abstract-only. The only intraday state-detection paper with an online step uses one month of 2012 equity data ([hendricks-2017](notes/hendricks-2017-intraday-market-states-temporal-clustering.md)). Evolutionary methods appear only as an optimiser for a clustering likelihood in that paper; I found no study that evolves regime definitions or regime-conditioned rules and tests them out of sample.
- **Not covered by a note.** Kritzman, Page and Turkington (2012), "Regime Shifts: Implications for Dynamic Strategies", is paywalled and was seen only in a search summary. Volatility-regime GARCH work on Bitcoin belongs to topic 14 and macro-variable sensitivity to topic 02. Three recent preprints were opened and set aside as weaker than the noted sources: an HMM-plus-tabular-RL allocation study on daily ETFs (arXiv:2605.27848) whose learned policy matched an equal-weight portfolio's Sharpe ratio with zero costs assumed; a genetic-algorithm parameter re-optimiser for 5-minute crypto strategies (arXiv:2510.07943), which adapts by periodic re-tuning and does not detect regimes; and an intraday day-classifier for Nasdaq futures (arXiv:2605.11423) in which none of eight directional rules passed out-of-sample tests.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. On SOL bars of 5 minutes to 1 hour, do regimes separate on anything besides volatility? Fit two- to four-state models and test equality of mean returns across states, as [ang-2011](notes/ang-2011-regime-changes-financial-markets.md) does.
2. Does a regime gate improve an existing directional signal net of fees? Compare the signal alone, with an HMM gate, with a jump-model gate, and with a plain realised-volatility threshold. The threshold is the baseline to beat; none of the sources tests against it.
3. What is the detection delay, in bars, of each detector on known events (liquidation cascades, large macro releases), and what does a false alarm cost? [chauvet-2005](notes/chauvet-2005-real-time-business-cycle-dating.md) shows the asymmetry between entering and leaving a stress regime; measure it for crypto.
4. Is there a distinct rebound state after liquidation cascades, as in [guidolin-2007](notes/guidolin-2007-asset-allocation-multivariate-regime-switching.md)'s crash-to-recovery transition, and is its sign predictable enough to trade?
5. Do perp-specific features (funding rate, open-interest change, basis, liquidation volume, cross-venue flow) improve regime classification over return-only features? [cortese-2023](notes/cortese-2023-cryptocurrency-returns-sparse-jump-model.md) points to activity and attention features at daily frequency.
6. Should the BTC-to-SOL lead-lag coefficient depend on regime, and in which direction? [joshi-2025](notes/joshi-2025-regime-specific-interdependencies-crypto-gmm-var.md) gives conflicting indications; repeat with SOL, online regime labels and Binance or Coinbase prices as the lead.
7. Do regimes estimated on large venues (Binance SOL perp, CME, BTC) carry over to Kalshi's SOL perp, given its low leverage cap, 8-hour funding and thin book? Regime labels from the leading venue may be the only usable ones until Kalshi has history.
8. Do changepoint features help a learned model after realistic costs when turnover is in the loss function, which [wood-2022](notes/wood-2022-slow-momentum-fast-reversion-changepoint.md) recommends and does not fully test?
9. How often must regime models be refitted intraday, and how unstable are the labels across refits? [hendricks-2017](notes/hendricks-2017-intraday-market-states-temporal-clustering.md) found 60-minute states less stable than 5- to 30-minute ones.
10. Can a reinforcement-learning or evolutionary search over regime-conditioned rules beat a hand-specified two-stage design out of sample? The available evidence ([asadi-2026](notes/asadi-2026-regime-aware-llm-expert-switching.md), [agakishiev-2025](notes/agakishiev-2025-regime-switching-reinforcement-learning-crypto.md)) does not show that it can. Any such test needs multiple seeds, a walk-forward split and a count of configurations tried.
