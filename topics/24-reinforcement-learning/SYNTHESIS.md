# Reinforcement learning for trading: synthesis

Sixteen sources, from the 1998 direct-reinforcement paper to preprints from October 2026. Short version for the bot: the literature supports borrowing RL's problem formulation (position in the state, costs and funding in the reward, risk-adjusted objective) and its evaluation discipline, supports RL for execution where the bot's orders change what happens next, and does not support the claim that an end-to-end deep RL agent reliably beats a forecast plus a cost-aware position rule on intraday crypto.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Formulation matters more than algorithm.** The results that hold up share three design choices: the previous position is an input to the policy, the cost of changing position is inside the reward, and risk enters either through the reward (differential Sharpe ratio, quadratic utility) or through volatility scaling of position. See [Moody and Saffell 1998](notes/moody-1998-reinforcement-learning-for-trading.md), [Zhang, Zohren and Roberts 2019](notes/zhang-2019-deep-reinforcement-learning-trading-futures.md), [Jiang, Xu and Liang 2017](notes/jiang-2017-deep-rl-portfolio-management-crypto.md) and [Borrageiro, Firoozye and Barucca 2022](notes/borrageiro-2022-recurrent-rl-crypto-agent-perpetual.md). For a perpetual, the per-step reward is `price change x previous position - (half-spread + fee) x |position change| - funding x position`.

2. **A small directional trader is a price taker, and that changes the problem.** When the agent's orders do not move prices, the reward of any action on any historical bar is computable, the policy can be trained by direct gradient ascent with no exploration or value function ([Jiang 2017](notes/jiang-2017-deep-rl-portfolio-management-crypto.md), [Moody 1998](notes/moody-1998-reinforcement-learning-for-trading.md)), replay is a valid counterfactual ([Levine et al. 2020](notes/levine-2020-offline-rl-tutorial-review-open-problems.md)), and multi-period credit assignment adds little beyond what the position-change cost already requires ([Ma 2025](notes/ma-2025-myopic-optimality-rl-portfolio-lose-money.md), a theory argument). RL's distinctive machinery earns its keep only where actions change future state: inventory, fills, queue position, impact, liquidation risk ([Zhu and Cai 2026](notes/zhu-2026-ai-equity-crypto-profitability-limits.md)).

3. **Execution is the cleanest use case.** [Nevmyvaka, Feng and Kearns 2006](notes/nevmyvaka-2006-reinforcement-learning-optimized-trade-execution.md) is the only grade-A positive result here: a tabular policy on (time left, inventory left, spread, cost to cross) cut cost by a quarter to a half against a fixed limit price on a six-month test with simulated impact. [Hambly, Xu and Yang 2021](notes/hambly-2021-recent-advances-rl-finance.md) catalogue the follow-up work and the standard benchmarks (TWAP, VWAP, submit-and-leave).

4. **Results are fragile across seeds and hyperparameters.** [Henderson et al. 2017](notes/henderson-2017-deep-reinforcement-learning-that-matters.md) show two sets of five seeds with identical settings giving statistically different results. In crypto: information ratios from 0.22 to 1.76 across 250 initializations of one agent ([Borrageiro 2022](notes/borrageiro-2022-recurrent-rl-crypto-agent-perpetual.md)); 11 of 100 execution agents collapsing to never trading, and conclusions that change between 30 and 100 seeds ([Ardaiz, Budati and Habibnia 2026](notes/ardaiz-2026-mixture-experts-crypto-execution-failure-modes.md)); probability of backtest overfitting above 17% for a walk-forward-tuned PPO ([Gort et al. 2022](notes/gort-2022-drl-crypto-backtest-overfitting.md)).

5. **Standard backtest environments overstate performance.** Fill at the bar close or last trade, no spread, no self-impact and no competition for fills are the usual assumptions ([Jiang 2017](notes/jiang-2017-deep-rl-portfolio-management-crypto.md), [Zong et al. 2024](notes/zong-2024-macrohft-memory-context-rl-crypto.md), [Liu et al. 2020](notes/liu-2020-finrl-deep-rl-library-stock-trading.md)). [Noble, Rosenbaum and Souilmi 2026](notes/noble-2026-bridging-reality-gap-lob-simulation.md) show P&L flattening or declining once self-impact and latency races are modeled; [Byrd, Hybinette and Balch 2019](notes/byrd-2019-abides-high-fidelity-market-simulation.md) make the same point with an agent-based simulator. [Ma 2025](notes/ma-2025-myopic-optimality-rl-portfolio-lose-money.md) and [Zhu and Cai 2026](notes/zhu-2026-ai-equity-crypto-profitability-limits.md) add look-ahead leakage inside the training procedure itself.

6. **Off-the-shelf deep RL does not work on minute-level crypto.** The strongest evidence is in the baseline rows of a paper promoting a new method: DQN, DDQN and PPO lost money on BTC, ETH, DOT and LTC at a 0.02% fee, and a simple order-imbalance rule beat all three on three of the four coins ([Zong 2024](notes/zong-2024-macrohft-memory-context-rl-crypto.md)). In [Gort 2022](notes/gort-2022-drl-crypto-backtest-overfitting.md) every agent lost 35% to 59% over two months. In [Ardaiz 2026](notes/ardaiz-2026-mixture-experts-crypto-execution-failure-modes.md) no learned execution policy beat TWAP.

7. **Non-stationarity is handled by procedure, not by a better network.** Rolling retraining ([Moody 1998](notes/moody-1998-reinforcement-learning-for-trading.md), [Zhang 2019](notes/zhang-2019-deep-reinforcement-learning-trading-futures.md)), continuous online updates with a performance gate ([Borrageiro 2022](notes/borrageiro-2022-recurrent-rl-crypto-agent-perpetual.md)), regime-specialized sub-policies ([Zong 2024](notes/zong-2024-macrohft-memory-context-rl-crypto.md)), and hard-coded risk switches outside the policy ([Liu 2020](notes/liu-2020-finrl-deep-rl-library-stock-trading.md), [Gort 2022](notes/gort-2022-drl-crypto-backtest-overfitting.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Value-based versus direct policy methods.** Moody and Saffell find direct reinforcement beats Q-learning; Borrageiro et al. argue value functions mis-assign reward after jumps; Zhang et al. find DQN beats policy gradient and A2C. The common thread is turnover: in each study the method that traded less won. No source read here settles the ranking on intraday crypto.
- **Does RL add anything over supervised forecast plus a one-step rule?** [Ma 2025](notes/ma-2025-myopic-optimality-rl-portfolio-lose-money.md) says no for price takers, on theory alone. Moody and Saffell say RL beats supervised training once costs are included, citing their own earlier work (not opened). None of the sources runs a clean head-to-head on intraday crypto with matched features, costs and seeds. This is the main open empirical question for the project.
- **The positive crypto results are single-path.** [Jiang 2017](notes/jiang-2017-deep-rl-portfolio-management-crypto.md) (three 50-day windows, zero-slippage fills), [Borrageiro 2022](notes/borrageiro-2022-recurrent-rl-crypto-agent-perpetual.md) (one instrument, no frozen holdout, long bias in a rising market), [Zong 2024](notes/zong-2024-macrohft-memory-context-rl-crypto.md) (about 20 trades per test window). None is replicated; only Borrageiro models shorting and funding; none models liquidation.
- **Overfitting gates are not validated.** The PBO test in [Gort 2022](notes/gort-2022-drl-crypto-backtest-overfitting.md) accepted an agent (TD3) that then performed worst. PBO measures rank stability among trials, not profitability.
- **Simulators.** Agent-based ([Byrd 2019](notes/byrd-2019-abides-high-fidelity-market-simulation.md)) and calibrated point-process ([Noble 2026](notes/noble-2026-bridging-reality-gap-lob-simulation.md)) approaches are both equity-specific. No source read shows a policy trained in an open simulator transferring to live crypto trading. Multi-agent RL environments exist (ABIDES-Gym, arXiv:2110.14771; JAX-LOB, arXiv:2308.13289; ABIDES-MARL, arXiv:2511.02016; JaxMARL-HFT, arXiv:2511.02136; existence confirmed, not read).
- **Offline RL and imitation in trading are thin.** [Levine 2020](notes/levine-2020-offline-rl-tutorial-review-open-problems.md) gives the general theory; [Hambly 2021](notes/hambly-2021-recent-advances-rl-finance.md) notes the methods had not been tailored to finance. A decision-transformer trading preprint exists (arXiv:2411.17900, not read).
- **Learned position sizing.** In the credible studies sizing is a rule (volatility targeting in Zhang et al.; a tanh output with a risk-aversion constant in Borrageiro et al.). No source shows a learned sizing policy beating volatility targeting.
- **Venue gap.** Nothing covers Kalshi, low-leverage CFTC perps, or the SOL perp specifically; SOL appears only as one of ten spot coins in Gort et al. Transfer across assets and cold start are named as open problems by Hambly et al.
- **Execution evidence conflicts by era and market.** Nevmyvaka (2006, equities, positive) against Ardaiz (2026, BTC, negative under frictionless replay). An October 2025 preprint (RL-Exec, arXiv:2511.07434; abstract seen only) reports PPO beating TWAP on BTC-USD replays with injected impact, so the sign depends on the environment specification.

- **Coverage limits of this synthesis.** Supervised prediction (topics 22 and 23) and evolutionary search (topic 25) are covered elsewhere. Works not noted here: Deng et al. 2017 (deep direct RL, IEEE TNNLS; cited by Gort et al., paywalled, not opened); and, with existence confirmed on arXiv but not read, EarnHFT (arXiv:2309.12891), FinRL-Meta (arXiv:2211.03107), FinRL Contests (arXiv:2504.02281), Balch et al. 2019 on replay versus interactive simulation (arXiv:1906.12010) and Sadighian 2019 on crypto market making (arXiv:1911.08647). Web search was unavailable during this session, so discovery of 2025 to 2026 work relied on arXiv q-fin.TR monthly listings from September 2025 to October 2026.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. **RL versus forecast-plus-rule.** On SOL perp 5-minute data with Kalshi fees, spread and 8-hour funding, does a direct-reinforcement policy beat a supervised return forecast fed into a one-step no-trade-band rule, using the same features, walk-forward windows and at least several dozen seeds? Ma's argument predicts no difference in mean and higher variance for RL.
2. **Cross-venue transfer and cold start.** Train on Binance or Bybit SOL perp history, deploy on Kalshi. How much Kalshi data is needed before fine-tuning helps, and does a policy trained elsewhere survive Kalshi's different fees, funding caps and leverage limits when each reward term is recomputed under Kalshi rules?
3. **What is the policy actually earning?** Decompose P&L into direction, execution cost and funding as Borrageiro et al. do. Does a learned SOL policy reduce to "long plus carry"? Compare against volatility-matched long, carry-only and momentum baselines.
4. **Reward choice.** Differential Sharpe ratio, quadratic utility and volatility-scaled profit on identical data: which gives the lowest turnover and best net result, and how sensitive is each to its one free parameter?
5. **Action space under a low leverage cap.** Discrete {-1, 0, 1} versus continuous position. Zhang et al. found the continuous agent over-traded; does that repeat at 5 minutes?
6. **Seed dispersion.** What is the spread of out-of-sample information ratio across seeds for a SOL agent, and how many seeds are needed before a configuration change can be called real?
7. **Does PBO predict anything?** Compute PBO for each model family at each refit and check whether low-PBO families do better in the following window on SOL.
8. **Regime conditioning.** Do policies specialized by trend and volatility tercile, or one policy with those labels as inputs, beat a single unconditioned policy in walk-forward tests? This links to the macro-regime topics.
9. **Execution on a thin book.** With small live orders on Kalshi, measure fill rates for resting orders and realized cost of crossing. Does a Nevmyvaka-style table beat "cross immediately" after fees? This needs live exploration data; a replay backtest cannot answer it.
10. **Where replay breaks.** Plot backtested P&L against order size relative to Kalshi touch depth, with and without a self-impact term, to find the size above which price-taker assumptions fail.
11. **Net-reward gate.** Does switching off when the running net reward is negative improve results, or does it only lag regime changes?

## Reading order

1. [Moody and Saffell 1998](notes/moody-1998-reinforcement-learning-for-trading.md): the formulation everything else builds on.
2. [Borrageiro, Firoozye and Barucca 2022](notes/borrageiro-2022-recurrent-rl-crypto-agent-perpetual.md): the same idea on a crypto perpetual with funding; closest template for the bot.
3. [Zhang, Zohren and Roberts 2019](notes/zhang-2019-deep-reinforcement-learning-trading-futures.md): reward and action design for deep RL on futures.
4. [Henderson et al. 2017](notes/henderson-2017-deep-reinforcement-learning-that-matters.md): why single-run results mean little.
5. [Gort et al. 2022](notes/gort-2022-drl-crypto-backtest-overfitting.md): overfitting in crypto deep RL and a gate against it.
6. [Zong et al. 2024](notes/zong-2024-macrohft-memory-context-rl-crypto.md): current academic state of the art at one-minute crypto; read the baseline rows.
7. [Ma 2025](notes/ma-2025-myopic-optimality-rl-portfolio-lose-money.md) and [Zhu and Cai 2026](notes/zhu-2026-ai-equity-crypto-profitability-limits.md): the case against end-to-end RL for price takers, and the reporting standard for perps.
8. [Nevmyvaka, Feng and Kearns 2006](notes/nevmyvaka-2006-reinforcement-learning-optimized-trade-execution.md), then [Ardaiz, Budati and Habibnia 2026](notes/ardaiz-2026-mixture-experts-crypto-execution-failure-modes.md): the positive and negative poles of RL execution.
9. [Levine et al. 2020](notes/levine-2020-offline-rl-tutorial-review-open-problems.md): what a log of past data can and cannot tell a learned policy.
10. [Noble, Rosenbaum and Souilmi 2026](notes/noble-2026-bridging-reality-gap-lob-simulation.md) and [Byrd, Hybinette and Balch 2019](notes/byrd-2019-abides-high-fidelity-market-simulation.md): simulators and the sim-to-real gap.
11. [Hambly, Xu and Yang 2021](notes/hambly-2021-recent-advances-rl-finance.md): reference survey for theory and for execution and market-making formulations.
12. [Jiang, Xu and Liang 2017](notes/jiang-2017-deep-rl-portfolio-management-crypto.md) and [Liu et al. 2020](notes/liu-2020-finrl-deep-rl-library-stock-trading.md): widely cited, useful for formulation and tooling, weak as evidence.
