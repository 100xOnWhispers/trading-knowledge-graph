# 21 Cross-asset lead-lag and spillovers: synthesis

Scope: predictability of one asset from another at short horizons. BTC and ETH leading altcoins including SOL; US equity indices and crypto during US hours; return and volatility spillovers and connectedness; estimating lead-lag from asynchronous ticks (Hayashi-Yoshida, Hawkes, lagged cross-correlation); cross-sectional lead-lag networks; machine-learning, reinforcement-learning and evolutionary approaches to these relations; and how stable any of it is. Sixteen source notes; three are abstract-only and flagged. Same-asset lead-lag across venues belongs to topic 05 and macro announcements to topic 02.

Coverage note: the session's shared web-search budget was exhausted for most of the run, so discovery relied on a handful of searches plus known identifiers. Gaps are listed at the end of the second section.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Liquidity decides who leads.** The more actively traded instrument leads the less active one: index futures lead stocks, and leaders have shorter inter-trade durations, narrower spreads and larger turnover ([Huth and Abergel 2014](notes/huth-2014-high-frequency-lead-lag-empirical-facts.md)). In crypto, the speed with which a coin responds to BTC rises with its trade count, with a cross-sectional correlation of about 0.5 between responsiveness and log trade count across roughly 370 Binance coins ([Kurihara and Matsumoto 2026](notes/kurihara-2026-price-transmission-bitcoin-altcoins.md)); "the more frequently traded cryptocurrencies are, the faster is the synchronization of their fluctuations" ([Watorek et al. 2021](notes/watorek-2021-multiscale-characteristics-cryptocurrency-market.md)).

2. **Among liquid coins the BTC and ETH lead is at most about a minute, and mostly contemporaneous at one-minute sampling.** ETH and LTC show no lagged response to BTC at one minute; only the least-traded coins do ([Kurihara and Matsumoto 2026](notes/kurihara-2026-price-transmission-bitcoin-altcoins.md)). Across 80 liquid Binance coins the lag-zero correlation with BTC and ETH is much larger than the one-minute-lag correlation in either direction, with a small asymmetry in favour of BTC and ETH leading ([Kwapien, Watorek and Drozdz 2021](notes/kwapien-2021-cryptocurrency-market-consolidation.md)). Information flow from small coins back to BTC is not detected in either study.

3. **Lead-lag lives at short horizons and is strongest after large moves of the leader.** In European equities cross-correlation is essentially gone within tens of seconds to five minutes, and both the asymmetry and the correlation rise with the size of the leader's move ([Huth and Abergel 2014](notes/huth-2014-high-frequency-lead-lag-empirical-facts.md)). In US equities lagged cross-asset order flow helps at one minute and loses its advantage quickly as the horizon lengthens (Cont, Cucuringu and Zhang 2023; see the topic 07 note [cont-2023](../07-order-book-imbalance/notes/cont-2023-cross-impact-order-flow-imbalance.md)).

4. **A visible lead is not a tradable edge for a taker.** A 60% hit rate on the lagger's next mid-quote move turns into a loss of about 3 bps per trade once the spread is paid ([Huth and Abergel 2014](notes/huth-2014-high-frequency-lead-lag-empirical-facts.md)). Topic 05 reaches the same conclusion for cross-venue crypto.

5. **Crypto has become close to a one-factor market, and that factor is now tied to US equities.** The first principal component of major-coin returns went from 36% of variance in 2015-16 to above 80% in 2019-20, and total return connectedness from about 25% to 80% ([Aslanidis, Bariviera and Perez-Laborda 2020](notes/aslanidis-2020-cryptocurrencies-becoming-more-interconnected.md)); intra-crypto correlation rose again from late 2020 to mid-2021 ([Kwapien et al. 2021](notes/kwapien-2021-cryptocurrency-market-consolidation.md)). Bitcoin's beta to US equities on 30-minute US-hours returns moved from about zero to above one around late 2020 ([Drexler, Guettler and Sun 2026](notes/drexler-2026-bitcoin-rising-beta-equities.md)); minute-data cross-correlations with the S&P 500 appeared in 2020 and strengthen in turbulent periods ([Watorek et al. 2021](notes/watorek-2021-multiscale-characteristics-cryptocurrency-market.md)).

6. **Cross-asset structure is unstable.** Lead-lag clusters in US equities have low year-to-year persistence and the derived signal decays after 2012 ([Bennett, Cucuringu and Reinert 2022](notes/bennett-2022-lead-lag-detection-network-clustering.md)); learned, time-varying leader sets beat precomputed correlation graphs ([Zhou et al. 2025](notes/zhou-2025-deltalag-dynamic-lead-lag-patterns.md)); Bitcoin's dominance as a spillover source faded as other coins grew ([Aslanidis et al. 2020](notes/aslanidis-2020-cryptocurrencies-becoming-more-interconnected.md)); the network hub at hourly scales switches among coins ([Kwapien et al. 2021](notes/kwapien-2021-cryptocurrency-market-consolidation.md)); the equity beta has a break in 2020 ([Drexler et al. 2026](notes/drexler-2026-bitcoin-rising-beta-equities.md)); weekend-crypto-to-Monday-equity volatility spillover exists only from late 2023 ([Ebadollahyvahed and Umeorah 2026](notes/ebadollahyvahed-2026-weekend-crypto-volatility-monday-risk.md)).

7. **Measurement must handle asynchronous trading.** Correlations collapse at fine sampling (the Epps effect) and previous-tick resampling manufactures a lead for the more active asset. The Hayashi-Yoshida estimator on raw ticks, its lead/lag ratio, and surrogate tests avoid this ([Huth and Abergel 2014](notes/huth-2014-high-frequency-lead-lag-empirical-facts.md); topic 05 note on [Hoffmann, Rosenbaum and Yoshida 2013](../05-price-discovery-cross-venue/notes/hoffmann-2013-lead-lag-non-synchronous-data.md)); Hawkes cross-excitation models express the same facts in closed form ([Bacry et al. 2013](notes/bacry-2013-microstructure-noise-mutually-exciting-point-processes.md)); wavelet versions show that leadership can differ by time scale ([Hayashi and Koike 2020](notes/hayashi-2020-multi-scale-lead-lag-high-frequency.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Sign of intraday cross-predictability in crypto.** [Guo, Sang, Tu and Wang 2024](notes/guo-2024-cross-cryptocurrency-return-predictability.md) report that lagged returns of other coins predict a coin's return, interpreted as slow diffusion (positive spillover). [Jia, Wu, Yan and Liu 2023](notes/jia-2023-seesaw-effect-cross-predictability-cryptocurrencies.md) report a negative intraday lead-lag from large coins to others. Both are peer-reviewed, both claim profits after costs, and both were read as abstracts only, so frequency and sample could not be compared. The two can be reconciled if the sign depends on bar length and on whether the target's own contemporaneous move is controlled for, but that is a conjecture.
- **Does BTC lead at all for a coin like SOL?** The popular view says yes. The one-minute evidence says only for illiquid coins (Kurihara and Matsumoto) or only weakly and possibly as an autocorrelation artefact (Kwapien et al., who say they "cannot answer the question decisively"). A daily cross-predictability network study opened but not noted (Guo, Hardle and Tao, arXiv 2108.11921) states that larger market capitalization "does not imply a leading position" and that BTC, LTC and ETH predict fewer coins than smaller ones. A thesis seen only in a search snippet (Southampton, not opened) reportedly finds Bitcoin often lagging at tick level. Sub-minute evidence for SOL was not found.
- **Equities leading crypto intraday.** The sources show strong contemporaneous linkage in US hours (Drexler et al.; Watorek et al.), not a measured lead. No study estimating lead-lag between equity index futures and BTC or SOL at seconds to minutes was found. The reverse direction exists at the weekly calendar scale (Ebadollahyvahed and Umeorah; they cite Mourey et al. 2025 for returns, not opened). The NASDAQ-as-driver belief is contradicted by Drexler et al., who find the broad market matters and the NASDAQ adds little once it is controlled for.
- **Connectedness is not lead-lag.** Diebold-Yilmaz measures ([Diebold and Yilmaz 2014](notes/diebold-2014-network-topology-variance-decompositions.md)) attribute contemporaneous co-movement to both assets; "net transmitter" often means "more volatile". The weekend study shows a calendar-specific spillover that the rolling connectedness index on the same assets misses.
- **Machine learning, reinforcement learning, evolutionary methods.** Evidence is thin and mostly not crypto-intraday. Sparse linear models (LASSO, adaptive LASSO) are the common choice for cross-asset features (Cont et al.; Guo et al.; Jia et al.). Gradient boosting on two lagged returns works on illiquid coins in one-week tests (Kurihara and Matsumoto). Deep cross-attention beats statistical graphs on daily US equities with a two-year test (Zhou et al.). Tuned ML models "do not consistently outperform" HAR for volatility spillover (Ebadollahyvahed and Umeorah). The one RL source ([Yang and Malik 2024](notes/yang-2024-reinforcement-learning-pair-trading-dynamic-scaling.md)) has one pair, one test month, and results that flip sign across RL algorithms. The one evolutionary source ([Danilo, Jamhamed and Martin 2026](notes/danilo-2026-pairs-trading-cryptoassets-genetic-algorithms.md), abstract only) reports an out-of-sample Sharpe of 0.69 per pair, against 1.53 in the earlier backtest version. No source applies RL or evolutionary search to cross-asset lead-lag proper. Topics 23, 24 and 25 cover these methods in general.
- **Costs and realism.** Only Huth and Abergel net out the spread. Kurihara and Matsumoto and Yang and Malik charge a flat 0.02% fee and no spread on illiquid instruments. Bennett et al. and Zhou et al. report gross figures.
- **SOL and Kalshi.** No source analyses SOL's lead-lag with BTC, ETH or equities, and none covers a regulated low-leverage US perp. Everything about SOL here is inference from the liquidity law.
- **Not covered or not accessible.** Hayashi and Yoshida (2005) could not be opened (publisher bot protection); it is cited through the notes above. Koutmos (2018) was read as an abstract only (Bitcoin the dominant spillover source among 18 coins, spillovers rising and spiking around news) and not noted. The IMF note by Iyer (2022) was read as a summary page only. Full texts of Guo et al., Jia et al. and Danilo et al. were blocked. Not searched for lack of budget: transfer-entropy studies of crypto, Hawkes-based lead-lag in crypto (Da Fonseca and Zaatour for the model), Cartea, Cucuringu and Jin on lead-lag portfolios, high-frequency crypto connectedness with SOL in the sample, and practitioner research from exchanges or data vendors.

Design implication for a minutes-to-hours SOL bot: the literature does not support "trade SOL on BTC's last move" on liquid venues at one minute or longer. Cross-asset information is better used as (a) factor state, meaning the trend, flow and volatility of the crypto market factor and of US equities, weighted by rolling betas; (b) very short-horizon flow features from leaders, for timing; and (c) a lag monitor on the Kalshi venue itself, where the liquidity law predicts a lag behind the offshore market that the major venues no longer show.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. At bar lengths of 10 s, 1, 5, 15 and 60 minutes, what is the coefficient of SOL's next-bar return on BTC's and ETH's last-bar return after controlling for SOL's own last-bar return? Is it positive (slow diffusion), negative (seesaw) or zero, and does the sign change with bar length, venue (Binance versus Kalshi) and session?
2. What are the HY lead/lag ratio and peak lag for BTC-perp versus SOL-perp on Binance, and for Binance SOL versus Kalshi SOL, by hour of day? Does the Kalshi lag show the predicted dependence on Kalshi trade count?
3. Does conditioning on large leader moves (say BTC moves above 10 to 20 bps in a few seconds) produce a SOL response on Kalshi that exceeds Kalshi's spread and fee, when the unconditional lead does not?
4. Is there any lead from ES or NQ futures to BTC and SOL at 1 s to 5 min in US hours, around the cash open, close and data releases? Use HY and the wavelet scale map; compare ES, NQ and YM given the Dow-versus-NASDAQ finding.
5. Does a rolling equity beta gate improve equity-futures features, that is, do they predict SOL only when the trailing beta and R-squared are high?
6. Does cross-asset OFI from Binance BTC, ETH and SOL predict Kalshi SOL mid returns at 1 to 5 minutes, and how fast does the edge decay with horizon compared with the equity result?
7. How persistent is the lead-lag network among the top 50 perps at 5-minute bars? Measure week-to-week adjusted Rand index of Hermitian clusters and the half-life of any cluster-flow signal for SOL.
8. Does the first-eigenvalue share at 10-minute scale mark regimes in which SOL-specific signals (on-chain, funding, positioning) work better or worse?
9. Does crypto variance during the US overnight or weekend predict SOL volatility and Kalshi spread at the next US open, and does Monday's equity open feed back into SOL direction?
10. Method tests flagged by the project scope: (a) a DeltaLag-style attention model with SOL as the only target at 1 to 15 minute bars, against a LASSO baseline, walk-forward; (b) an RL policy with the SOL-versus-basket residual as state, evaluated over many months and seeds, against fixed thresholds; (c) evolutionary search over lag sets, bar lengths and thresholds for the leader-move feature, with a held-out period the search never sees, reporting the in-sample to out-of-sample shrinkage. None of these has direct supporting evidence in this topic's sources.

2. Topic 05 note on [Hoffmann, Rosenbaum and Yoshida 2013](../05-price-discovery-cross-venue/notes/hoffmann-2013-lead-lag-non-synchronous-data.md), then [Hayashi and Koike 2020](notes/hayashi-2020-multi-scale-lead-lag-high-frequency.md): the lag estimator and its multi-scale extension.
