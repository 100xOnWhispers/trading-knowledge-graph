## Key insight
Issued with the BTCPERP order, this statement sets the US listing framework: crypto perps like the approved bitcoin contract can follow that precedent, while perps on other asset classes should go through prior Commission review under Regulation 40.3 instead of self-certification. It is non-binding guidance, which means the framework that the Kalshi SOL perp sits in can change without rulemaking.

## Method
A three-page general statement of policy issued under 5 U.S.C. 553(b)(A), adopted May 29, 2026 and published June 3, 2026. It recounts the regulatory history, explains why perps differ structurally from dated futures, and states the Commission's view on the filing route.

## Data and sample
Legal and policy text; no data.

## Reported results
- History cited: CFTC staff requests for comment of April 21, 2025 on "Perpetual" style derivatives (Release 9069-25) and on 24/7 trading (Release 9068-25); the President's Working Group report of July 30, 2025 recommending access to perpetual contracts through regulated intermediaries.
- Structural point: for a cash-settled dated future the manipulation analysis concerns one moment, expiry; for a perpetual "the reference must be reliable at every funding interval, without interruption, for as long as the contract remains active".
- Filing route: perps on asset classes "not contemplated in the Order" should be submitted for review under Regulation 40.3. Footnote 5 names agricultural products, precious metals, equity securities and narrow-based security indexes, and says perps are "likely particularly ill-suited for agricultural products".
- Status: the statement "does not impose any obligation on any person", does not amend the Act or regulations, and leaves open later policy statements, guidance or rulemaking.
- Voting summary: Chairman Selig voted in the affirmative; no Commissioner voted in the negative.
- The Federal Register search that located this document also returned a June 25, 2026 CFTC request for comment on extending standard futures to 24/7 trading and on perps referencing energy commodities; that document was not opened.

## Signal construction
Not a signal. It is used for venue-risk assessment and for dating regulatory regime changes that may shift liquidity between US venues.

## Relevance to SOL perpetuals at minutes to hours
The statement explains the pattern in the CFTC filing index: Kalshi's crypto perps, including SOL, were self-certified in early June 2026, while its gold, silver, platinum, copper, equity-index, Treasury and crude-oil perps were first submitted for approval under Regulation 40.3 between July and October 2026. For the bot, the practical consequences are that contract terms such as the funding cap and margin are exchange rules that can be amended by filing, sometimes with ten business days' notice or less under emergency authority, and that competing US venues can list near-identical SOL perps under the same precedent. A model trained on one regime of fees, leverage and competition should expect breaks.

## Caveats
A policy statement binds no one and gives no detail on leverage limits, customer margin, liquidation standards or disclosure. It does not address decentralised venues or foreign boards of trade. Whether SOL qualifies as a "digital commodity" for this purpose depends on separate SEC and CFTC guidance of March 2026 cited in the BTCPERP order, which was not opened in this session.
