# Topic 17 synthesis: practitioner frameworks for reading liquidity and trader activity

Scope: market profile and volume profile, VWAP and anchored VWAP, auction market theory, footprint and delta, absorption and exhaustion, stop hunts and liquidity sweeps, liquidation heatmaps, Wyckoff, ICT and smart-money concepts, support and resistance, round numbers. Each framework is stated as a computable rule and then set against whatever evidence could be found. Machine-learning, reinforcement-learning and evolutionary work was searched for within this scope, as requested.

A limit on this synthesis: web search was rate-limited for most of the session, so coverage rests on sources reachable by known address plus seven successful searches. Nineteen notes were written. Three are abstract-only and one is secondary; they are marked as such.

## What the literature agrees on

**1. Orders cluster at round prices, and the clustering has structure.** In a dealer's book of 9,667 FX stop-loss and take-profit orders, about 8.7% sat on rates ending in 00 against 1% under uniformity. Take-profit orders sit on the round number; stop-loss buys sit just above it and stop-loss sells just below ([osler-2003](notes/osler-2003-currency-orders-exchange-rate-dynamics.md)). Round-price clustering is also reported for Bitcoin ([urquhart-2017](notes/urquhart-2017-price-clustering-bitcoin.md), abstract only) and, per that abstract's citation of Harris (1991), for equities.

**2. Price behaves differently at such levels, by a small amount.** Published FX support and resistance levels produced a 60.8% bounce rate against 56.2% for random levels ([osler-2000](notes/osler-2000-support-resistance-intraday-exchange-rates.md)). After crossing a round number, dollar-mark moved about 0.7 basis points further in 15 minutes than after crossing an arbitrary one (0.061% against 0.054%), and the continuation effect lasted at least two hours while the reversal effect lasted under thirty minutes ([osler-2002](notes/osler-2002-stop-loss-orders-price-cascades.md)). Algorithmic levels from local extremes show excess bounce probability that rises with the number of earlier bounces, in 2002 UK equity ticks ([garzarelli-2011](notes/garzarelli-2011-memory-effects-support-resistance.md)) and in 2018 one-minute FX, equity and commodity data ([chung-2021](notes/chung-2021-support-resistance-levels-evidence-behaviour.md)), with decay over a few hours.

**3. Levels are a proxy for resting liquidity.** Support and resistance coincide with depth peaks in the limit order book ([kavajecz-2004](notes/kavajecz-2004-technical-analysis-liquidity-provision.md), abstract only). In crypto the book is public, so a bot can measure what charts only approximate.

**4. Forced, price-contingent flow can be exploited and overshoots.** Theory shows that traders who know of a forced liquidation sell ahead of it and buy back, causing overshoot that is larger when the market is thin and predators are few ([brunnermeier-2005](notes/brunnermeier-2005-predatory-trading.md)). This is the defensible core of the "stop hunt" idea.

**5. The baseline is never 50%.** Random levels bounce 56% of the time in Osler's data; neutral control zones win 43% to 55% depending on drift and direction in the placebo studies ([algolego-2026-order-block](notes/algolego-2026-order-block-statistics-placebo.md)). Every credible test in this topic compares against matched placebo levels or shuffled returns.

**6. The practitioner constructs are precisely definable.** Point of control, value area, initial balance and single prints ([sierrachart-tpo](notes/sierrachart-2026-tpo-profile-value-area-calculation.md)); bid and ask volume, delta and diagonal imbalance ([sierrachart-numbers-bars](notes/sierrachart-2026-numbers-bars-footprint-delta.md)); fair value gaps, order blocks, structure breaks and "liquidity" clusters ([joshyattridge-2026](notes/joshyattridge-2026-smart-money-concepts-python.md)); springs and upthrusts ([bogomazov-2026](notes/bogomazov-2026-wyckoff-method-tutorial.md)). None of these four sources offers evidence.

## Where it disagrees or is weak

**Statistical effect versus profit.** No source shows a level-based rule that is profitable after costs. The one study that converts levels into returns finds no excess over buy-and-hold in US stocks ([zapranis-2012](notes/zapranis-2012-horizontal-support-resistance-levels.md), abstract only). Osler's effects are about one basis point. The bounce studies use bands about one typical price increment wide.

**Crypto evidence is thin and partly negative.** Bitcoin prices cluster, yet the abstract reports no significant return pattern after round numbers ([urquhart-2017](notes/urquhart-2017-price-clustering-bitcoin.md)). I found no peer-reviewed intraday test of round numbers, support and resistance, volume profile or VWAP levels on crypto perpetuals.

**ICT and smart-money concepts fail the only controlled tests found.** Fair value gaps: pooled real-minus-placebo hit rate of -0.04 points, interval about -1.2 to +1.1, across crypto, FX and index futures ([algolego-2026-fvg](notes/algolego-2026-fair-value-gap-statistics-placebo.md)). Order blocks: mean of 23 estimates -0.87 points, all pooled intervals including zero ([algolego-2026-order-block](notes/algolego-2026-order-block-statistics-placebo.md)). These are pseudonymous practitioner studies, not peer-reviewed, but their design is better than anything offered in favour of the concepts. No academic test of ICT concepts was found.

**VWAP as a level rests on one in-sample equity backtest.** A stop-and-reverse rule on QQQ around session VWAP reports a Sharpe ratio of 2.1 with zero slippage, about fifteen trades a day and a 17% hit rate ([zarattini-2023](notes/zarattini-2023-vwap-holy-grail-day-trading.md)). The institutional reason VWAP matters in equities is benchmarking ([madhavan-2002](notes/madhavan-2002-vwap-strategies-benchmark.md), secondary), which has no confirmed counterpart in crypto. The crypto VWAP paper found is about execution, not direction ([genet-2025](notes/genet-2025-deep-learning-vwap-execution-crypto.md)).

**Market profile, volume profile, auction theory, footprint patterns, absorption, exhaustion and Wyckoff have no evidence in the sources read.** They have definitions and a large following. Signed trade flow, the raw input of footprint charts, does have a formal literature in topics 07 and 08; the specific practitioner patterns built on it do not.

**Does repeated testing strengthen or weaken a level?** Garzarelli and Chung find that more prior bounces mean a higher bounce probability. Chung also finds that for Brent a single prior bounce predicts penetration at longer windows. The sign is asset- and horizon-dependent.

**Sweep-and-reverse versus break-and-run.** Osler's data favour continuation after a crowded level is crossed. Wyckoff springs and ICT sweeps claim reversal after a brief breach. Predatory-trading theory allows both in sequence: overshoot, then reversal once forced flow is spent. No source read here tests the conditional claim (breach plus fast reclaim).

**Liquidation heatmaps.** Not covered by a new note here. The only source in the repository is a vendor article with a visual comparison and no statistics: [manrique-2025](../04-open-interest-liquidations/notes/manrique-2025-liquidation-heatmaps-market-bias.md) in topic 04. The claim that liquidation clusters act as price magnets is untested.

**Machine learning, reinforcement learning, evolutionary methods.** Within this topic's scope the cupboard is nearly bare. A search for learning-based work using volume-profile, market-profile or support-and-resistance features on crypto returned only general RL trading papers and one TradingView script: an online logistic model with a UCB bandit on value-area breaks, with no reported performance ([aetheredge-2026](notes/aetheredge-2026-adaptive-volume-profile-bandit.md)). The deep-learning VWAP paper is an execution study. One of the general RL papers the search returned, MacroHFT, is noted in topic 24 ([zong-2024](../24-reinforcement-learning/notes/zong-2024-macrohft-memory-context-rl-crypto.md)); it does not use profile or level features. Learning-based work on technical or order-flow inputs sits in neighbouring topics: reinforcement learning in topic 24, evolutionary search in topic 25 (for example [neely-1997](../25-evolutionary-algorithms/notes/neely-1997-genetic-programming-fx-technical-analysis.md) and [allen-1999](../25-evolutionary-algorithms/notes/allen-1999-genetic-algorithms-technical-trading-rules.md)), automated chart-pattern detection by kernel regression in topic 16 ([lo-2000](../16-oscillators-technical-rules/notes/lo-2000-foundations-technical-analysis.md)), and genetic programming on order-flow inputs in topics 07 and 08 ([funie-2014](../07-order-book-imbalance/notes/funie-2014-genetic-programming-swarm-hft-rules.md), [manahov-2021](../08-trade-flow-toxicity/notes/manahov-2021-efficiency-bitcoin-genetic-programming.md)). Because level definitions have many free parameters, any search over them needs the data-snooping controls of topic 26 ([white-2000](../26-backtest-overfitting/notes/white-2000-reality-check-data-snooping.md), [bailey-2015](../26-backtest-overfitting/notes/bailey-2015-probability-backtest-overfitting.md)) in addition to the placebo design used here.

How to use the table with learning methods. Treat every row as an event generator with a common label (accept or reject by triple barrier fixed in advance). Offer the features to a supervised model with walk-forward validation and an ablation against a model that sees only recent return, volatility, signed flow and depth. Use a contextual bandit only for the fade-or-follow decision per level family. If an evolutionary search is used to tune level definitions (windows, bands, anchors), put the real-minus-placebo difference, not the raw hit rate, in the fitness function, and include a synthetic no-edge run.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. Which price grid do SOL orders cluster on (cents, whole dollars, 5 or 10 dollar multiples), measured on Binance and Bybit trade prices and resting depth, and on Kalshi?
2. Does Osler's pair of effects exist on SOL at 1 to 15 minutes: excess reversal on first touch of a round price and excess continuation after a confirmed cross, both against matched non-round prices? Are they larger than fees?
3. Does bounce probability at rolling-extreme levels rise or fall with touch count on SOL, and over what window does it decay to the shuffled-return baseline?
4. Do chart-defined levels add anything once visible depth peaks and recent signed flow are in the model?
5. Is there any anchor (00:00 UTC, funding timestamps, US open, last liquidation flush) for which price relative to VWAP predicts the next 15 to 60 minutes beyond a same-length moving average?
6. Do POC and value-area edges of the prior UTC day or prior funding window attract or repel price beyond what a volatility band around VWAP explains?
7. Breach plus reclaim within k bars at range boundaries and equal-highs clusters: reversal, continuation, or nothing, against breaches without reclaim?
8. Do estimated liquidation clusters on Hyperliquid and modelled clusters for Binance raise the probability that price reaches them, or only the size of the move once it does? Does overshoot revert as predatory-trading theory predicts?
9. Do the placebo results for fair value gaps and order blocks replicate on SOL across periods of opposite drift?
10. Is the absorption proxy (extreme delta with little price progress) at a pre-defined level followed by reversal more often than matched high-volume bars?
11. With low leverage on Kalshi, do stop and liquidation effects appear on Kalshi's own tape at all, or only through the lead venues?
