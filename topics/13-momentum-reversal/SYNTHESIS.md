# Topic 13: Momentum, trend following and short-horizon reversal

Scope: time-series momentum and trend following in futures and crypto, intraday momentum, short-term reversal and mean reversion from minutes to days, the horizon at which momentum flips to reversal, breakout and moving-average rules as trend measures, pairs and statistical arbitrage where it informs directional mean reversion, and machine-learning, reinforcement-learning and evolutionary methods applied to these signals. Oscillator rules (topic 16) and DeMark (topic 15) are excluded.

Twenty-one notes. Sixteen were read in full text; four are abstract-only and one is secondary, and they are marked as such. The web-search budget for the session ran out part-way through, so coverage of 2025 to 2026 crypto-specific work, of overreaction after jumps, and of evolutionary methods applied to crypto is thinner than intended. The last section lists work that should be added.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Trends exist at slow horizons in diversified futures, and the standard construction is simple.** Sign of the trailing return times inverse volatility is profitable across 58 instruments from 1985 to 2009 ([Moskowitz et al. 2012](notes/moskowitz-2012-time-series-momentum.md)) and in every decade from 1880 to 2016 ([Hurst et al. 2017](notes/hurst-2017-century-trend-following.md)). The per-market Sharpe ratio is modest, about 0.4 in the long sample; the headline Sharpe above 1 comes from diversification across dozens of markets. A single-asset bot should plan around the per-market number.

2. **Crypto trends too, but on a much shorter clock.** Bitcoin's own daily and weekly returns predict the next days and weeks ([Liu and Tsyvinski 2018](notes/liu-2018-risks-returns-cryptocurrency.md)). In the cross-section, momentum peaks at roughly two weeks and turns to reversal beyond about a month ([Dobrynskaya 2023](notes/dobrynskaya-2023-cryptocurrency-momentum-reversal.md), secondary access). Breakout systems on Bitcoin do best with 5 to 30 day windows ([Zarattini et al. 2025](notes/zarattini-2025-catching-crypto-trends.md)). A machine-learned combination of 28 trend indicators predicts weekly coin returns net of costs and in large coins ([Fieberg et al. 2025](notes/fieberg-2025-trend-factor-cryptocurrency-returns.md)). The horizon map in traditional assets (continuation for 1 to 12 months, reversal after) is compressed in crypto to continuation for days to about two to four weeks, reversal after.

3. **Liquidity and volume decide whether a move continues or reverts.** Illiquid coins show daily reversal, the largest coins show daily momentum ([Zaremba et al. 2021](notes/zaremba-2021-short-term-reversal-momentum-liquidity-crypto.md), abstract-only). Reversal profits concentrate after low-volume moves and in small coins, and are zero or negative for large coins and high-volume moves ([Bianchi et al. 2022](notes/bianchi-2022-trading-volume-liquidity-provision-crypto.md)). The same rule appears in equities stat-arb, where down-weighting high-volume moves in the contrarian signal restored performance ([Avellaneda and Lee 2010](notes/avellaneda-2010-statistical-arbitrage-us-equities.md)), and in Bitcoin intraday momentum, which is three to four times stronger after high-volume opening sessions ([Shen et al. 2022](notes/shen-2022-bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum.md)). Across four independent settings: high relative volume means follow, low relative volume means fade.

4. **Inside the day, momentum and reversal coexist at different lags.** In Bitcoin the first half-hour of a session predicts the last half-hour positively while the second-to-last half-hour predicts it negatively ([Shen et al. 2022](notes/shen-2022-bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum.md)); the sign also depends on jumps, macro announcements and liquidity ([Wen et al. 2022](notes/wen-2022-intraday-return-predictability-crypto.md), abstract-only). At the 30-minute lag in 24-hour FX the dominant structure is negative first-order autocorrelation ([Neely and Weller 2001](notes/neely-2001-intraday-technical-trading-fx.md)). Pairs strategies in crypto find mean reversion at 5-minute and hourly bars that is absent in daily data ([Fil and Kristoufek 2020](notes/fil-2020-pairs-trading-cryptocurrency-markets.md), abstract-only).

5. **Intraday predictability is real but small relative to costs.** Break-even one-way costs are about 1 basis point for evolved half-hourly FX rules ([Neely and Weller 2001](notes/neely-2001-intraday-technical-trading-fx.md)) and 3 to 10 basis points for Bitcoin intraday momentum ([Shen et al. 2022](notes/shen-2022-bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum.md)). Deep-learning trend models lose their advantage above 2 to 3 basis points unless turnover is penalised in training ([Lim et al. 2019](notes/lim-2019-deep-momentum-networks.md)). Break-even cost per trade is the statistic every candidate signal should report.

6. **Volatility scaling and blending horizons are the robust parts of the recipe.** Every profitable construction in this folder sizes positions inversely to volatility and most average several look-backs ([Hurst et al. 2017](notes/hurst-2017-century-trend-following.md); [Zarattini et al. 2025](notes/zarattini-2025-catching-crypto-trends.md); [Fieberg et al. 2025](notes/fieberg-2025-trend-factor-cryptocurrency-returns.md)).

7. **For learned and evolved rules, the method that holds up is conservative.** Train on the trading objective with costs inside it ([Lim et al. 2019](notes/lim-2019-deep-momentum-networks.md); [Zhang et al. 2019](notes/zhang-2019-deep-reinforcement-learning-trading.md)); use a selection period separate from training and an inflated cost during search ([Neely et al. 1997](notes/neely-1997-genetic-programming-technical-rules-fx.md)); shrink over a pre-specified indicator set instead of searching for rules ([Fieberg et al. 2025](notes/fieberg-2025-trend-factor-cryptocurrency-returns.md)); and always compare against a low-order linear benchmark, which matched genetic programming on intraday FX ([Neely and Weller 2001](notes/neely-2001-intraday-technical-trading-fx.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Is time-series momentum predictability or just drift plus volatility timing?** [Huang et al. 2020](notes/huang-2020-time-series-momentum-is-it-there.md) show the pooled t-statistic of the original study fails bootstrap critical values, that 47 of 55 assets show no significant own-return predictability, and that a "long if the historical mean is positive" strategy matches TSMOM. This matters more for crypto than for futures: the crypto samples (2011 to 2022) have enormous positive drift, most crypto trend results are long-only or long-biased ([Zarattini et al. 2025](notes/zarattini-2025-catching-crypto-trends.md); [Detzel et al. 2021](notes/detzel-2021-learning-predictability-technical-analysis-bitcoin.md), abstract-only), and the benefit is often "avoided the crash" rather than "predicted the sign". None of the crypto trend papers noted here runs the Huang et al. benchmark.

- **Does crypto momentum still exist?** A later working paper by Dobrynskaya and Dubrovskiy (2022, HSE WP 86/FE/2022, abstract seen in this session, no note) states that the momentum premium "has vanished in the recent years". Fieberg et al. find a trend factor through May 2022 that subsumes plain momentum. Han, Kang and Ryu, "Time-Series and Cross-Sectional Momentum in the Cryptocurrency Market: A Comprehensive Analysis under Realistic Assumptions", reportedly find time-series momentum strong but concentrated in bull markets and cross-sectional momentum weak; the paper could not be opened (HTTP 403) and that description comes from a search summary only.

- **Reversal at daily frequency: tradeable or bid-ask bounce?** Equal-weighted reversal returns above 1% a day ([Bianchi et al. 2022](notes/bianchi-2022-trading-volume-liquidity-provision-crypto.md)) sit in the smallest, least tradeable pairs; value-weighted returns in high-volume pairs are negative. For a liquid coin such as SOL, unconditional daily reversal is not supported.

- **Intraday evidence is thin and old.** The two full-text intraday crypto sources are Bitcoin spot through 2020 and BitMEX 2018 to 2019. Nothing peer-reviewed in this folder tests SOL, perpetual contracts on current venues, or data after 2022 at intraday frequency. The one 2026 paper on perpetuals at multi-hour bars ([Bui and Nguyen 2026](notes/bui-2026-adaptive-trend-following-crypto.md)) is grade C with clear overfitting channels.

- **Machine-learning results have not been shown on crypto or intraday data.** The deep momentum, changepoint and reinforcement-learning papers ([Lim et al. 2019](notes/lim-2019-deep-momentum-networks.md); [Wood et al. 2021](notes/wood-2021-slow-momentum-fast-reversion.md); [Zhang et al. 2019](notes/zhang-2019-deep-reinforcement-learning-trading.md)) all use daily data on 50 to 88 futures from one vendor and come from one research group. Their advantage relies on pooling many assets; a single-asset intraday application is an extrapolation.

- **Evolutionary search: works slow, fails fast.** Genetic programming found profitable daily FX rules out of sample over 15 years ([Neely et al. 1997](notes/neely-1997-genetic-programming-technical-rules-fx.md)) and nothing net of costs at half-hourly frequency ([Neely and Weller 2001](notes/neely-2001-intraday-technical-trading-fx.md)). No credible evolutionary-rule study on crypto was located in this session.

- **Pairs-trading back-tests in crypto are not credible as stated.** Sharpe ratios near 7 with maker rebates supplying a quarter to a third of profit ([Tadi and Kortchemski 2021](notes/tadi-2021-dynamic-cointegration-pairs-trading-crypto.md)) and 11.61% a month at 5-minute bars beside "underperforms benchmarks" ([Fil and Kristoufek 2020](notes/fil-2020-pairs-trading-cryptocurrency-markets.md)) both point to edges that live inside the spread.

- **Conflicts of interest.** The trend-following canon is largely written by AQR principals; the best crypto breakout paper is co-authored by a trading-research firm.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. **Horizon map.** Regress SOL forward returns over h on trailing returns over k for k and h from 5 minutes to 30 days. Where does the coefficient change sign? The literature predicts reversal at the shortest lags (minutes to about an hour), continuation from several hours to about two weeks, and reversal beyond a month. This single table determines which signals in the table above apply at which bar size.
2. **Volume conditioning.** Does the sign of the next-bar return coefficient on the last-bar return flip with relative volume (aggregated across Binance, Bybit, OKX, Hyperliquid and Coinbase)? Test at 5-minute, 30-minute and 4-hour bars.
3. **Drift versus timing.** Apply the Huang et al. benchmark: does a SOL trend rule beat volatility-scaled long, and does its short leg make money on its own? Repeat on the 2022 bear market and on 2025 to 2026 separately.
4. **Session anchors.** Is there an open-predicts-close effect in SOL around the US equity session, the CME session, or the 8-hour funding timestamps? Does it survive the all-in cost on Kalshi?
5. **Residual versus raw mean reversion.** Is the half-life of SOL's residual against BTC and ETH short and stable enough to trade, and does the residual s-score predict SOL's own return (the directional leg) or only the spread?
6. **Venue-specific reversal.** Do Kalshi-only price moves (not matched on the large venues) revert faster than moves that originate on the large venues? The liquidity results suggest yes; no source tests it.
7. **Break-even cost per signal.** For each candidate, compute the one-way cost that zeroes the out-of-sample return and compare it with Kalshi fees plus half-spread plus expected funding over the holding period.
8. **Leverage cap interaction.** Volatility targeting asks for more notional when volatility is low. How often does Kalshi's leverage cap bind, and how much of the back-tested benefit of volatility scaling is lost when it does?
9. **Regime switch between trend and reversion.** Does a cheap online changepoint or volatility-regime feature improve the choice between the trend and mean-reversion components on hourly SOL data, as it does on daily futures?
10. **Learned versus linear.** Does any LSTM, reinforcement-learning or evolved rule beat a ridge regression on the same features after costs, on a walk-forward basis, when trained on a panel of liquid perpetuals and tested on SOL?
11. **Bar size for trend rules.** Is there an interior optimum between 1-hour and daily bars after costs, as the 2026 perpetuals paper claims, when the bar size is chosen on a period disjoint from the test?

## Neighbouring work not noted here

Mentioned for orientation; none of these has a note in this folder, and unless stated they were not opened in this session, so details should be confirmed before use.

- Gao et al. (2018) on market intraday momentum in equity ETFs and Baltussen et al. (2021) on intraday momentum across futures: the origins of the design in Shen et al., cited there.
- Elaut et al. (2018): intraday momentum in FX attributed to liquidity provision, cited by Shen et al.
- Wood, Giegerich, Roberts and Zohren, "Trading with the Momentum Transformer" (arXiv 2112.08534; abstract and results summary opened): an attention-based successor to the Deep Momentum Network that reports a further Sharpe improvement over the LSTM and better behaviour in the 2020 crash.
- Han, Kang and Ryu on crypto momentum under realistic assumptions (liquidation risk, costs): blocked, high priority to obtain.
- Caporale and Plastun; Borgards and Czudaj: overreaction after large daily moves in crypto. Seen only in search summaries, which report that overreactions are detectable but not profitably exploitable after costs.
- Allen and Karjalainen (1999) on genetic algorithms for equity index trading rules; Garleanu and Pedersen on trading momentum and reversal optimally under costs (cited by Moskowitz et al.).
- Oscillator rules such as RSI and MACD as tested rules belong to topic 16; several machine-learning papers here use MACD as an input feature only.
