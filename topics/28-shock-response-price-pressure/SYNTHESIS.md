# Continuation or reversal after large moves: synthesis

Scope: the bot's core directional question. After a sharp move over minutes to hours, does price continue (information) or revert (price pressure)? Four sub-areas: (1) theory and classic evidence on transient versus permanent moves; (2) crypto studies of overreaction after large moves, jumps and flash crashes; (3) return paths after liquidation bursts and validation of liquidation-heatmap levels; (4) perp state (funding, premium, open interest, long/short ratios, funding timestamps) as a predictor of the perp's own return, including machine-learning, reinforcement-learning and evolutionary studies that use those inputs.

Seventeen source notes, all opened on 2026-11-22. Fifteen were read in full text, one is abstract-only and one is a practitioner article. Coverage is uneven and the reader should know why. The session's shared web-search budget was exhausted for most of the run, so discovery relied on the task's leads, on direct fetches of known open copies and on a small number of searches that got through. Sub-area 1 is well covered. Sub-area 2 is covered for 2013 to 2021 data. Sub-areas 3 and 4 remain thin: no study was found that measures return paths after liquidation bursts across many events, none that validates heatmap levels, and none that tests funding or open interest as a predictor of the perp's own intraday return with a proper out-of-sample design. Those gaps are findings in their own right and are listed at the end.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **A price move has a permanent part and a transitory part, and the transitory part is payment for absorbing flow.** Liquidity suppliers take the other side of urgent orders only at a concession and earn it back as price recovers. [Hendershott and Menkveld](notes/hendershott-2014-price-pressures.md) measure this directly on NYSE specialist inventories: pressure averages 49 basis points with a half-life of 0.92 days, 17 basis points and half a day for large stocks, 118 basis points and two days for small ones. Pressure differs across stocks far more than the spread does.

2. **Volume is the classic variable that separates the two parts, but its sign depends on why people traded.** [Campbell, Grossman and Wang](notes/campbell-1993-trading-volume-serial-correlation-returns.md) show that moves on high volume reverse more (coefficient on volume times return of -0.33, standard error 0.06, on daily index data). [Llorente, Michaely, Saar and Wang](notes/llorente-2002-dynamic-volume-return-relation.md) show the sign flips with information asymmetry: high-volume moves reverse in liquid large caps and continue in small, wide-spread names. Hedging or forced trades reverse; informed trades continue. This is the single most useful idea for the bot: a volume spike does not sign the trade until the flow is classified as forced or informed.

3. **Flow pressure is absorbed in minutes in a liquid market.** [Chordia, Roll and Subrahmanyam](notes/chordia-2005-speed-convergence-market-efficiency.md) find that lagged order imbalance predicts returns (continuation) for up to 30 minutes in 1996, 10 minutes in 1999 and 5 minutes in 2002, while returns alone are unpredictable even at 5 minutes. The window shrinks as markets get faster.

4. **Flow-driven moves are not always transitory.** In the 24-hour interdealer currency market, daily signed order flow explains 64% (DM) and 45% (yen) of exchange-rate changes and lagged flow shows no reversal ([Evans and Lyons](notes/evans-2002-order-flow-exchange-rate-dynamics.md)). Flow that reflects a real shift in what the market must hold moves price for good. Makarov and Schoar (topic 05) report the same strong flow-return relation for Bitcoin spot.

5. **The reward for fading moves rises in stress.** [Nagel](notes/nagel-2012-evaporating-liquidity.md) shows short-term reversal returns are strongly predicted by the VIX (coefficient 0.22, standard error 0.02; monthly adjusted R-squared 56%). When liquidity suppliers are constrained, overshoots are larger and so is the subsequent reversal. The same strategies suffered sharp, short losses when liquidity suppliers themselves had to delever.

6. **Liquidity crashes look alike across markets.** The 2010 Flash Crash ([Kirilenko et al.](notes/kirilenko-2017-flash-crash-high-frequency-trading.md)): a mechanical seller, intermediaries with tiny inventories passing contracts among themselves, volume about 8 times normal, a 5.1% fall in 13 minutes and full recovery in the next 23. Bitcoin on 19 May 2021 ([Baumgartner and Guettler](notes/baumgartner-2022-bitcoin-flash-crash-binance.md)): low at 13:09 UTC, about 22% above the low within an hour and about 35% within three and a half hours. In both cases the rebound began when forced flow stopped, and in the crypto case the affected venue's prices diverged from other venues by about seven times the normal gap and its data for the worst 40 minutes were back-filled.

7. **The size of a drop is set by flow relative to liquidity, with a concave law.** [Donier and Bouchaud](notes/donier-2015-why-do-markets-crash-bitcoin.md) show that the net selling in the April 2013 Mt. Gox crash was ordinary in bitcoin terms; buy-side liquidity was what had thinned. An illiquidity measure built from daily volatility and volume, `sigma_d / sqrt(V_d)`, tracks the order-book measure with R-squared of about 0.86. Amplitude is predictable; timing is not.

8. **In crypto the unconditional intraday tendency is reversal, and it grows with the size of the preceding move.** [De Nicola](notes/denicola-2021-intraday-behavior-bitcoin.md): first-order autocorrelation of -0.056 at one hour and -0.086 at two hours on Bitstamp 2015 to 2018, rising in magnitude to -0.19 after five-sigma one-hour bars and -0.40 after six-sigma two-hour bars. [Borgards and Czudaj](notes/borgards-2020-prevalence-price-overreactions-crypto.md) (abstract only) report overreactions as highly prevalent at intraday and daily frequencies in twelve coins. Kitron and Wengrowicz (2026, topic 08) find 15-minute sign reversal in 90% of 183 Binance pairs, concentrated after moves that taker flow pushed.

9. **The reversal edge is small next to costs.** Kitron and Wengrowicz's gross edge peaks near 1.3 basis points against a 5 basis point round trip. [Caporale and Plastun (2018)](notes/caporale-2018-price-overreactions-cryptocurrency.md) charge costs and find that fading daily overreactions loses and following them is indistinguishable from random. De Nicola's larger per-trade figures (0.3% to 0.7% after two- to four-sigma two-hour moves) are in-sample, before fees and from 2015 to 2018.

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Reversal or continuation after a large crypto move?** De Nicola, Borgards and Czudaj, and Kitron and Wengrowicz say reversal at 15 minutes to 4 hours. [Caporale and Plastun (2019)](notes/caporale-2019-momentum-after-abnormal-returns-crypto.md) say continuation through the rest of an abnormal day and often into the next morning. [Chevapatrakul and Mascia](notes/chevapatrakul-2019-detecting-overreaction-bitcoin-quantile.md) find continuation in the left tail of daily returns: after a sharp fall the next day's lower quantiles shift down. Kim and Hansen's quarter-hour study in topic 08 finds flow that reverses within half an hour and then predicts returns 4 to 12 hours ahead. A reading that reconciles these, not tested in any of them: sharp single-bar spikes are mostly pressure and revert, slow moves that build over many hours are mostly information and continue, and the average reversal is paid for by a fat left tail when a decline turns into a cascade. The 2019 Caporale result is also inflated by selection, since it conditions on the day ending as an extreme day.
- **Is the dislocation transitory when it is forced?** Theory says yes. The direct crypto evidence is two case studies in this topic and results held elsewhere: perps overshoot spot by about 3% of a large move and the gap closes within hours (Gornall et al., topic 03); liquidation trades on Hyperliquid pay roughly twenty times the voluntary half-spread, and loss-realizing closes are followed by reversion (Jia et al., topic 18); 88% of forced selling on 10 October 2025 landed in 30 minutes (Garcia Seuma, topic 04). All point to reversal after liquidation flow. None is an event study of signed returns after many liquidation bursts, net of the spreads that prevail in those minutes.
- **Liquidation heatmaps.** No statistical validation was found. Two searches returned only indicator descriptions, some of which disclaim predictive use, and the Glassnode article already held in topic 04. The claim that clusters attract price remains untested.
- **Funding as a predictor.** The popular view ([Tsentsura](notes/tsentsura-2026-funding-rates-predict-reversals-practitioner.md), grade D) is that extreme funding with high open interest precedes violent reversals "by days, not hours", supported by two episodes and no test. The only formal test opened, [Nimmagadda and Sasanka](notes/nimmagadda-2019-bitmex-funding-correlation-bitcoin.md), finds Granger causality in both directions at 8 hours and reports no sign. Kitron and Wengrowicz find funding, basis and the funding clock do not condition 15-minute reversal. Garcia Seuma finds no event-invariant early warning. Schmeling, Schrimpf and Todorov (topic 03, abstract only) report that high carry predicts crashes at a horizon that could not be confirmed, because the BIS file was again unreachable. The balance of evidence is that funding mostly echoes the recent price path and has no demonstrated short-horizon predictive content; the tail claim is open.
- **Open interest change relative to depth, and exchange long/short ratios.** No study found. Donier and Bouchaud supply the functional form for the first. Topic 18 holds one episode for the second.
- **Returns around funding timestamps.** Ruan and Streltsov (topic 03) document higher volume, wider spreads and more informed trading around settlement, not signed returns. The two Zhivkov (2026) papers on 26 venues, which appear to bear on hourly versus 8-hour venues, were blocked again. No signed-return study was found.
- **Machine learning, reinforcement learning and evolutionary methods.** One new study was found that feeds funding and premium to RL policies with a chronological test and costs: [Wang et al., M3FT](notes/wang-2026-m3ft-multi-agent-perpetual-futures-rl.md). Its numerical-only RL baselines all lost money out of sample (-0.85% to -9.06%), a contrarian Bollinger state did no better, and the proposed language-mediated variant's gains are within seed noise. It is daily, has no SOL and does not ablate the perp-state inputs. Kitron and Wengrowicz report that boosted trees and an MLP lose to a three-parameter model on short-horizon reversal. A search for genetic-programming or symbolic-regression work on funding, open interest or liquidation factors found none; the LLM factor-mining paper held in topic 25 (Huang et al.) does not appear to use them. No ML study in this scope shows that perp-state inputs add out-of-sample skill.
- **Age and breadth of the crypto evidence.** The overreaction studies end between 2017 and 2019, use spot data from one or a few venues, and are almost all Bitcoin. Nothing uses SOL.
- **Measurement traps that inflate results.** Outcomes defined by the maximum counter-move (Caporale 2018) show "reversal" and "continuation" at once. Close prices include bid-ask bounce at 1 to 5 minutes (about half of the equity reversal return is bounce in Nagel). Full-sample volatility thresholds leak the future. Prices from a venue in outage may not have been tradable or even real (Baumgartner).

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. **The base event study.** Define a shock as a volatility-scaled return over 5, 15, 60 and 120 minutes on a cross-venue SOL composite. Measure signed mid-to-mid returns over the next 5 minutes to 8 hours, by shock size and sign, with spreads and fees of the venue to be traded charged at the levels that prevail after shocks. Does De Nicola's size-dependent reversal exist in SOL in 2024 to 2026, and does anything survive costs?
2. **Forced or informed.** Split the same shocks by whether open interest fell (forced closing) or rose (new positioning), and by whether liquidation prints accompanied them. Theory predicts reversal in the first group and continuation in the second.
3. **Fast or slow.** Split by the speed of the move: one-bar spikes against drifts that accumulate over 6 to 24 hours. This tests the reconciliation of De Nicola with Caporale and Plastun.
4. **Liquidation bursts.** Using Hyperliquid's complete liquidation record for SOL, run the event study on bursts ranked by forced notional relative to book depth. Report mean and lower-quantile paths from 5 minutes to 4 hours. Compare perp against spot, as De Nicola proposes, to isolate the leverage effect.
5. **Heatmap levels.** Build the exact Hyperliquid liquidation map for SOL and test whether a cluster within x% raises the probability of price reaching it, against a null of distance-matched random levels, and whether returns reverse after a cluster is swept.
6. **Does volume sign anything in crypto?** Estimate the coefficient on `V * r` at 5 to 120 minutes for SOL, overall and around known news times. Is it negative on average, and does it turn positive in news windows?
7. **Stress scaling.** Does the post-shock reversal coefficient rise with lagged implied or realized volatility, as Nagel finds, and does the left-tail quantile fall with it, as the Chevapatrakul method would show?
8. **Funding and premium as conditioners.** With the return over the previous funding window controlled for, do funding level, funding change or funding at its cap alter the post-shock path at 1 to 8 hours? Test the tail (top and bottom 5%) separately, since the mean effect appears to be nil.
9. **Funding timestamps.** Signed returns in the 60 minutes before and after settlement, conditional on the sign and size of funding, on 8-hour venues against hourly venues. No study was found.
10. **Open interest change relative to depth.** Does `d(OI) / depth` or liquidatable notional over `V` predict the size of the next move through the square-root law, and does a realized move in excess of the implied one revert?
11. **Long/short ratios.** Do extremes in exchange account ratios predict anything at 1 to 8 hours once the past return is controlled for?
12. **Kalshi lag and dislocation.** After a shock on offshore venues, how does the Kalshi SOL perp's gap to the composite behave, and is its reversal slower, as the price-pressure cross-section (larger pressure and longer half-life in thinner markets) predicts?
13. **Learners.** Do perp-state features improve a simple conditional model out of sample, shown by ablation, before any RL policy is trained on them?

## Reading order

1. [campbell-1993](notes/campbell-1993-trading-volume-serial-correlation-returns.md) then [llorente-2002](notes/llorente-2002-dynamic-volume-return-relation.md): why some moves reverse and others continue.
2. [hendershott-2014](notes/hendershott-2014-price-pressures.md): how large and how long-lived price pressure is, and how to measure it.
3. [chordia-2005](notes/chordia-2005-speed-convergence-market-efficiency.md): how fast flow pressure is absorbed intraday.
4. [evans-2002](notes/evans-2002-order-flow-exchange-rate-dynamics.md): the counterweight, flow with permanent effect in a 24-hour market.
5. [nagel-2012](notes/nagel-2012-evaporating-liquidity.md): reversal as the price of liquidity, and its dependence on stress.
6. [kirilenko-2017](notes/kirilenko-2017-flash-crash-high-frequency-trading.md) and [baumgartner-2022](notes/baumgartner-2022-bitcoin-flash-crash-binance.md): anatomy of a liquidity crash in futures and in crypto.
7. [donier-2015](notes/donier-2015-why-do-markets-crash-bitcoin.md): move size as flow over liquidity.
8. [denicola-2021](notes/denicola-2021-intraday-behavior-bitcoin.md): the main crypto evidence for size-dependent intraday reversal.
9. [caporale-2018](notes/caporale-2018-price-overreactions-cryptocurrency.md), [caporale-2019](notes/caporale-2019-momentum-after-abnormal-returns-crypto.md) and [chevapatrakul-2019](notes/chevapatrakul-2019-detecting-overreaction-bitcoin-quantile.md): the contrary and qualifying evidence.
10. [borgards-2020](notes/borgards-2020-prevalence-price-overreactions-crypto.md): abstract-level pointer to the broadest intraday overreaction study.
11. [nimmagadda-2019](notes/nimmagadda-2019-bitmex-funding-correlation-bitcoin.md) and [tsentsura-2026](notes/tsentsura-2026-funding-rates-predict-reversals-practitioner.md): how little is established about funding as a predictor.
12. [wang-2026](notes/wang-2026-m3ft-multi-agent-perpetual-futures-rl.md): what RL on perp-state inputs achieves out of sample.

Then read, in other topics: Kitron and Wengrowicz (2026) and Kim and Hansen (2026) in topic 08 for current short-horizon reversal and flow-timing evidence; Jia et al. (2026) in topic 18 for post-liquidation markouts; Gornall et al. (2025) and Ruan and Streltsov (2024) in topic 03; Garcia Seuma (2026) and Lim (2026) in topic 04.
