# Oscillators and technical trading rules: what the evidence supports

Scope: RSI, stochastic, MACD, Bollinger Bands, CCI, Williams %R, moving-average crossovers, channel breakouts and their combinations, including rules found or combined by machine learning, reinforcement learning and evolutionary search. Eighteen notes; five rest on abstracts only and are marked as such in their front matter.

The short version: classical rules have shown real predictive content in young, trending markets, but the edge is small, concentrated in a few rule types, quick to decay, and usually smaller than taker fees at intraday frequency. The literature is more useful to this project for its testing methods than for any single indicator.

## What the literature agrees on

**1. The significance of a rule depends on how many rules were tried.** Sullivan, Timmermann and White show a rule with a stand-alone p-value of 0.04 on S&P 500 futures has a snooping-adjusted p-value of 0.90 once the 7,846-rule universe is accounted for ([sullivan-1999](notes/sullivan-1999-data-snooping-technical-trading-bootstrap.md)). Every serious later study adopts some version of this correction: Reality Check, SPA, stepwise tests, or false discovery rates ([bajgrowicz-2012](notes/bajgrowicz-2012-technical-trading-false-discoveries.md), [hsu-2016](notes/hsu-2016-technical-trading-foreign-exchange.md), [hudson-2021](notes/hudson-2021-technical-trading-cryptocurrencies.md), [bakker-2017](notes/bakker-2017-intraday-technical-bitcoin.md)).

**2. Profits decay after discovery.** In FX, filter and moving-average rules that earned 8% to 9% a year before 1990 earned about zero afterwards; less-studied rules decayed more slowly ([neely-2009](notes/neely-2009-adaptive-markets-foreign-exchange.md)). On the Dow, the best rule as of 1986 was insignificant in 1987 to 1996 ([sullivan-1999](notes/sullivan-1999-data-snooping-technical-trading-bootstrap.md)). In Bitcoin, no 5-minute rule was significant after 2014 once fees were charged ([bakker-2017](notes/bakker-2017-intraday-technical-bitcoin.md)), and the best daily rules lost money in the first half of 2018 ([hudson-2021](notes/hudson-2021-technical-trading-cryptocurrencies.md)). The survey evidence says the same for futures after the early 1990s ([park-2004](notes/park-2004-profitability-technical-analysis-review.md)).

**3. Young and hard-to-value markets show more predictability.** Nasdaq more than NYSE ([lo-2000](notes/lo-2000-foundations-technical-analysis.md)); emerging more than developed currencies ([hsu-2016](notes/hsu-2016-technical-trading-foreign-exchange.md), abstract); altcoins more than Bitcoin out of sample ([hudson-2021](notes/hudson-2021-technical-trading-cryptocurrencies.md)). Detzel et al. give a rational-learning model that predicts exactly this ([detzel-2021](notes/detzel-2021-learning-predictability-technical-analysis-bitcoin.md), abstract).

**4. Transaction costs decide the outcome at high turnover.** The best pre-cost 5-minute Bitcoin rule breaks even at 6.32 basis points one way, below the exchange's minimum fee; with 13 basis points, significant rules fall from 541 to 22 ([bakker-2017](notes/bakker-2017-intraday-technical-bitcoin.md)). An EMA crossover is unprofitable below roughly 30-minute bars at 0.1% per transaction ([mroziewicz-2026](notes/mroziewicz-2026-walk-forward-ema-intraday-bitcoin.md)). Evolved rules trade eight times as often as hand-picked ones and tolerate lower costs ([allen-1999](notes/allen-1999-genetic-algorithms-technical-trading-rules.md)).

**5. Trend and breakout rules do better than oscillators on daily crypto data.** Trading range breakout is the only rule to beat buy-and-hold on Sharpe ratio in daily Bitcoin, while RSI(70/30) and Bollinger(20,2) reversal rules significantly underperform ([gerritsen-2020](notes/gerritsen-2020-profitability-technical-rules-bitcoin.md)). Filters and channel breakouts lead in the 14,919-rule study ([hudson-2021](notes/hudson-2021-technical-trading-cryptocurrencies.md)).

**6. Rules reduce drawdown and volatility more reliably than they add return.** This appears in genetic-algorithm rules on the S&P 500 ([allen-1999](notes/allen-1999-genetic-algorithms-technical-trading-rules.md)), daily crypto rules ([hudson-2021](notes/hudson-2021-technical-trading-cryptocurrencies.md)) and hourly EMA rules ([mroziewicz-2026](notes/mroziewicz-2026-walk-forward-ema-intraday-bitcoin.md)).

**7. Machine learning does not remove the need for these controls.** Reinforcement-learning results swing from -35% to -56% on the validation scheme alone ([gort-2022](notes/gort-2022-deep-rl-crypto-backtest-overfitting.md)). A Reality Check across machine-learning and technical rules in crypto rarely finds significant excess returns ([anghel-2021](notes/anghel-2021-reality-check-ml-technical-crypto.md), abstract). Genetic search found a result on one test window and none on others ([allen-1999](notes/allen-1999-genetic-algorithms-technical-trading-rules.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

**Do classical rules work in crypto at all?** Hudson and Urquhart say yes, with 20% to 50% of rules surviving correction. Wei, with a similar universe and a different test, says the top rules are luck ([wei-2022](notes/wei-2022-crypto-technical-analysis-lucky-factors.md)). Anghel says significant excess returns are rare. Part of the gap is the test (stepwise and FDR methods find more survivors than the Reality Check), part is whether costs are charged, and part is that the positive studies end in 2017, at the top of a bull market, with profits concentrated in buy signals.

**The sign of oscillator signals depends on bar size.** On daily bars, fading overbought readings loses ([gerritsen-2020](notes/gerritsen-2020-profitability-technical-rules-bitcoin.md)). On 5-minute bars, contrarian Bollinger and RSI rules are the best performers before costs ([bakker-2017](notes/bakker-2017-intraday-technical-bitcoin.md)), and one-minute range breakouts are followed by reversal ([corbet-2019](notes/corbet-2019-technical-trading-rules-cryptocurrency-intraday.md)). Yet the one-minute MA state shows continuation in the same data. Nobody has mapped the horizon at which reversal gives way to continuation, and some of the short-horizon reversal is bid-ask bounce.

**Is the historical evidence real or selected?** Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron ([brock-1992](notes/brock-1992-simple-technical-trading-rules.md), abstract) and Sullivan et al. accept that rules worked before 1986; Bajgrowicz and Scaillet argue the profits could never have been captured ex ante or after costs. Hsu et al. find FX predictability over 45 years where Neely et al. find it gone by the 1990s. Park and Irwin's count of 58 positive studies out of 92 is exposed to publication bias.

**Why would rules work?** Three mechanisms are offered and rarely tested against each other: rational learning about hard-to-value assets (Detzel et al.); clustered stop-loss and take-profit orders that make support and resistance levels partly self-fulfilling ([osler-2003](notes/osler-2003-currency-orders-technical-analysis.md)); and compensation for risk, with rule returns loading on beta and volatility (Anghel). Each implies a different conditioning variable.

**Weak spots in coverage.**
- Almost no peer-reviewed work tests RSI, stochastic, CCI, Williams %R or Ichimoku on intraday crypto with costs. The only source here that does (Bakker) is a master's thesis on 2013 to 2017 spot data. The other use of these indicators at 5 minutes is as reinforcement-learning inputs (Gort et al.), which says nothing about each indicator's own value.
- No study in this folder uses perpetual futures, funding rates, or data after 2022 for rule testing. None tests SOL except as one of ten coins in a two-month reinforcement-learning window.
- Stochastic, CCI, Williams %R and Ichimoku have no dedicated evidence here at all. Gort et al. found most oscillators so correlated at 5 minutes that all but RSI, DX and the Ultimate Oscillator were pruned.
- Execution is assumed at bar closes with flat fees. No study models queue position, spread or slippage.

A reading of the table for design purposes: the slow items (breakout context, hourly EMA state, price-to-MA ratio, volatility conditioning) have the better evidence and tolerate fees; the fast oscillator items have a measurable gross effect that current evidence says a taker cannot keep. Oscillators are best treated as features for a model or as timing filters inside a slower directional view, with the method rows applied to whatever is built.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. **All-in cost against the 6 basis point benchmark.** What is the realized one-way cost (fee plus half-spread plus slippage) on the Kalshi SOL perp, as maker and as taker? Bakker's best intraday reversal rule broke even at 6.32 basis points; Mroziewicz and Ślepaczuk's hourly trend rule at about 40. This single number decides which rows of the table are worth building.
2. **Where does reversal turn into continuation?** Estimate the sign and size of return predictability from a Bollinger z-score and from RSI at bar sizes from 1 minute to 1 day on SOL perp mid-prices (not last trades, to remove bid-ask bounce). The literature implies a crossover between 5 minutes and 1 day but has not located it.
3. **Does lead-venue state predict the lagging venue?** Compute the rules on Binance, Bybit or Hyperliquid SOL perps and test them on Kalshi prices. A rule that is arbitraged away on the lead venue may still describe where a small venue's price goes next. No source here tests cross-venue rule transfer.
4. **Is short-horizon reversal still present after 2022?** Bakker found it gone net of fees after 2014 on spot Bitcoin. Repeat by calendar year on SOL perps with a stepwise SPA test.
5. **Do round numbers and liquidation clusters condition breakout outcomes?** Osler's mechanism predicts continuation after a round level is crossed and reversal on approach. Test on SOL using round dollar levels and estimated liquidation bands from large-venue open interest.
6. **Long/short asymmetry.** Crypto rule profits came from buy signals in 2010 to 2017. With symmetric shorting and funding on a perp, do sell signals carry any information on SOL, or should rules be used long-or-flat only?
7. **Are oscillators redundant with each other at intraday bars?** Gort et al. pruned most of them at a 0.6 correlation threshold. Repeat on SOL: if RSI, stochastic, Williams %R and CCI are one factor, the library needs one of them.
8. **Does a model on continuous indicator values beat threshold rules?** Compare a boosted-tree model on raw RSI, band z-score and price-to-MA ratios with the equivalent discrete rules, under the same Reality Check that includes every model variant tried.
9. **Exposure-matched benchmarks.** For every candidate, compare against random entries with the same average position, holding time and turnover, and regress returns on SOL's own return and realized volatility (Anghel's risk-premium point).
10. **Rule half-life.** Using rolling select-then-trade windows (Bajgrowicz and Scaillet's persistence design), how long does a rule selected on the last N weeks keep a positive net return on SOL? Mroziewicz and Ślepaczuk's result suggests re-selection windows of one to four weeks; this should be measured, not assumed.
11. **Does funding change the trend-rule calculus?** None of the sources charges 8-hour funding. Trend rules will tend to be long when funding is positive, so the cost falls on the profitable side.
