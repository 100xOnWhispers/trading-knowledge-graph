# 05 Price discovery between spot, perps and venues: synthesis

Scope: where crypto price discovery happens (spot versus perpetuals, venue versus venue, centralized versus on-chain), lead-lag at seconds to minutes, arbitrage frictions, how a small or new venue tracks leaders, and how index and mark prices are built. Sixteen source notes; three are abstract-only and are flagged as such. Cross-asset lead-lag (BTC leading SOL) belongs to topic 21 and is not covered here.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Price discovery is concentrated in the largest, most active venue, and that venue is an offshore perpetual.** For bitcoin in 2019-2020 the leaders were perpetuals and futures on BitMEX, Huobi and OKEx, with CME futures and US spot reacting ([Alexander and Heck 2020](notes/alexander-2020-price-discovery-bitcoin-unregulated-markets.md), abstract only). By early 2021 the Binance USDT-margined perpetual was the main volatility transmitter and Binance and Huobi derivatives the strongest sub-second leaders ([Alexander, Heck and Kaeck 2021](notes/alexander-2021-binance-bitcoin-volatility-transmission.md); [Albers et al. 2021](notes/albers-2021-fragmentation-price-formation-cross-impact.md)). In 2026 Binance still leads Hyperliquid in 29 of 29 assets including SOL ([Nayyar 2026](notes/nayyar-2026-hyperliquid-lags-binance-lead-lag.md)) and in essentially every hourly window for BTC ([Lim 2026](notes/lim-2026-binance-leads-wallets-anticipate.md)).

2. **Smaller, newer, access-restricted or slower venues follow.** Bybit and FTX perps were strong laggards in 2021 (Albers et al.); Bybit and Huobi were volatility receivers (Alexander, Heck and Kaeck); the newly launched CME and CBOE futures followed spot in 2017-2018 ([Baur and Dimpfl 2019](notes/baur-2019-price-discovery-bitcoin-spot-futures.md), abstract only); Polymarket's BTC quotes follow Binance within a few hundred milliseconds ([Young 2026](notes/young-2026-openmarket-polymarket-binance-dataset.md)). Nothing in the set shows a small venue leading on average. The working assumption that the Kalshi SOL perp is a price taker is well supported by analogy, though no source measures Kalshi directly.

3. **The lags are short: tens of milliseconds to about a second between liquid venues.** Reported figures are about 100 ms or less (Lighter behind Binance), 600-800 ms (Hyperliquid behind Binance), about 347 ms (Polymarket quote response), and 500 ms prediction horizons with R-squared of 10-37% (Albers et al.). These are far below the minutes-to-hours horizon of the bot.

4. **Centralized books lead on-chain AMMs.** Binance leads Uniswap v2 for ETH on every date tested, and CEX-DEX arbitrage is often unprofitable after gas ([Plazuelo Pascual et al. 2025](notes/plazuelo-2025-price-discovery-cryptocurrency-markets.md)).

5. **Cross-venue price gaps are driven by venue-specific order flow and by frictions on arbitrage capital, and they predict relative returns.** A common order-flow factor explains most of the common price move; idiosyncratic flow explains the venue's deviation, which then reverts relative to other venues ([Makarov and Schoar 2020](notes/makarov-2020-trading-arbitrage-cryptocurrency-markets.md)). Regulated derivatives that ease shorting and capital movement increase cross-venue synchronicity ([Augustin, Rubtsov and Shin 2023](notes/augustin-2023-impact-derivatives-spot-bitcoin-futures.md), abstract only). Fees shape who leads: low-fee venues lead, high-fee/high-rebate venues lag (Albers et al.).

6. **Measurement needs care.** Information share must be estimated at fine sampling or its bounds are uninformative ([Hasbrouck 1995](notes/hasbrouck-1995-one-security-many-markets.md)); IS and CS reward low noise as well as speed, so the information leadership share is the better "who moves first" metric ([Putnins 2013](notes/putnins-2013-price-discovery-metrics-measure.md)); tick-level lead-lag should use the shifted Hayashi-Yoshida estimator on raw timestamps ([Hoffmann, Rosenbaum and Yoshida 2013](notes/hoffmann-2013-lead-lag-non-synchronous-data.md)); venues must be aligned on event time, since feed latency alone (about 479 ms median for Hyperliquid in Lim 2026) can manufacture a lead.

7. **Kalshi's anchor is a US spot index, not the offshore perp.** Kalshi crypto perps fund against a CF Benchmarks real-time index with a per-second premium, back-weighted over each 8-hour interval, a 0.01% deadband and a 2% cap ([Kalshi 2026 filing](notes/kalshi-2026-nearperp-perpetual-contract-terms.md)); the Solana index is an order-book-weighted mid of Coinbase, Kraken, Gemini, LMAX Digital, Bitstamp and Crypto.com ([CF Benchmarks 2026](notes/cfbenchmarks-2026-cme-cf-real-time-indices-methodology.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Spot versus derivatives.** Baur and Dimpfl find spot led the new CME/CBOE futures; Alexander and Heck find offshore derivatives lead everything; Plazuelo Pascual et al. find CME futures lead Binance spot only weakly (Hasbrouck shares of 0.52-0.56 on four days and 0.158 on a crash day, with Gonzalo-Granger saying "neither"). The conflict is mostly about which derivative (a thin, restricted new future versus a deep offshore perp), sampling frequency, and sample period. The practical reading: leadership follows volume and access, not instrument type.
- **Leadership under stress.** [Shen et al. 2026](notes/shen-2026-misguided-price-discovery-overshooting.md) show that on January 3, 2024 the Binance perp first responded perversely and then overshot (response above 1.25), so magnitude-based metrics credited it with leadership while an accuracy-based metric (IPES) credited spot. Plazuelo Pascual et al. see spot lead on August 5, 2024. The "perp leads" consensus is a calm-market result.
- **Does a visible lag imply an edge?** Albers et al. get high R-squared for laggards yet negative taker PnL at default fees; Young gets a clear lag yet a walk-forward model that cannot beat the lagging venue's own mid. Neither supports a naive "copy Binance onto the small venue" strategy for a participant without top-tier fees and latency.
- **Venue averages versus participants.** Lim finds Hyperliquid follows Binance on average, but a persistent minority of wallets anticipate Binance (half-to-half rank correlations around +0.5). One preprint, one asset, 29 days.
- **SOL-specific evidence is nearly absent.** Only Nayyar includes SOL (as one of 29 assets, with no SOL-specific number in the text). A Zenodo working paper by Divyasshree N (2025; landing page read, no note written) claims Solana DEXs lead Binance for SOL, but on two days of 1-minute bars; treat as an untested hypothesis. Solana's fast, cheap blocks make the Ethereum AMM evidence a poor guide.
- **Kalshi-specific evidence is absent.** No source measures Kalshi's perp lag, basis or information share. The SOL perp filing was not retrieved; index and funding terms are inferred from the NEAR and BNB filings. A search snippet of Kalshi's product page (not opened) mentioned 3.5x maximum leverage and funding three times a day.
- **Machine learning, reinforcement learning, evolutionary methods.** The cross-venue ML evidence here is two studies with linear or logistic models (Albers et al.; Young). I found no study applying reinforcement learning or evolutionary/genetic methods specifically to cross-venue lead-lag or leader-follower execution; the web-search budget for the session ran out before a dedicated search could be completed, so this is a gap in coverage, not evidence of absence. Borrageiro, Firoozye and Barucca (2022, arXiv 2201.04699), an RL agent on the BitMEX perpetual, is single-venue and belongs to the RL topic.
- **Weak evidence grades.** The 2026 Hyperliquid results are a preprint and an industry blog. Three classic crypto papers were read as abstracts only.

Design implication for a minutes-to-hours directional bot: compute directional features (order flow, momentum, liquidation and positioning signals from other topics) on Binance and the other large venues, execute on Kalshi, and treat the raw sub-second lag as a cost-control tool (do not post stale quotes, do not chase a move the makers have already priced) instead of as the alpha source.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. What is the Kalshi SOL perp's lag behind the Binance SOLUSDT perp, by Hayashi-Yoshida peak and by single-clock event study, and how does it vary by US session, volatility and Kalshi depth? Is it seconds (potentially usable) or sub-second (not usable at ordinary latency)?
2. What are the Hasbrouck bounds and ILS for Kalshi versus Binance, versus Hyperliquid, and versus the CF Solana index? Does Kalshi ever lead, for instance during US-hours news or around its own funding times (12:00 AM, 8:00 AM, 4:00 PM ET)?
3. Does the Kalshi mid minus a cross-venue reference mean-revert, with what half-life, and does Kalshi-specific signed flow explain it, as Makarov and Schoar found for spot venues? Is the deviation large enough to cover Kalshi fees and spread?
4. Following Albers et al., what R-squared do cross-venue features achieve for Kalshi's next 1 s, 10 s, 1 min and 5 min mid return, and at which horizon does predictability net of costs peak? Benchmark against Kalshi's own mid, strictly walk-forward (Young's protocol).
5. How does the CF Solana real-time index behave relative to the Binance perp: lag, basis, and sensitivity to the USDT/USD rate? Does the index-versus-offshore gap predict Kalshi's funding or its next-interval drift?
6. In liquidation cascades on offshore SOL perps, does Kalshi (low leverage, spot-index anchored) overshoot less? If so, is fading the offshore overshoot on Kalshi profitable, as the Shen et al. spot-leads-in-stress result suggests?
7. Do Solana DEX prices or Hyperliquid SOL wallets carry lead information for SOL that the BTC and ETH evidence does not show? Test the wallet lead-score method on SOL at 10 s to 10 min horizons.
8. Does the Kalshi lag shrink over time as the venue matures (the Augustin et al. integration effect)? Track monthly from launch.
9. Untested method ideas flagged by the user's scope: nonlinear learners (trees, neural networks) on the Albers feature set, which the authors themselves list as future work; a reinforcement-learning execution policy that decides when to take or post on the lagging venue given leader-venue state; evolutionary or genetic search over cross-venue feature transforms. None has supporting evidence in this topic's sources.

## Reading order

1. [Hasbrouck 1995](notes/hasbrouck-1995-one-security-many-markets.md): the information share framework everything else uses.
2. [Putnins 2013](notes/putnins-2013-price-discovery-metrics-measure.md): why IS and CS can mislead for a noisy small venue, and the ILS fix.
3. [Makarov and Schoar 2020](notes/makarov-2020-trading-arbitrage-cryptocurrency-markets.md): arbitrage frictions, common versus venue-specific order flow.
4. [Alexander and Heck 2020](notes/alexander-2020-price-discovery-bitcoin-unregulated-markets.md) and [Baur and Dimpfl 2019](notes/baur-2019-price-discovery-bitcoin-spot-futures.md): the two sides of the regulated-venue-versus-offshore debate (abstracts only).
5. [Alexander, Heck and Kaeck 2021](notes/alexander-2021-binance-bitcoin-volatility-transmission.md): Binance USDT perp as the volatility source.
6. [Albers et al. 2021](notes/albers-2021-fragmentation-price-formation-cross-impact.md): the most actionable paper; cross-venue features, linear ML, fees, live test.
7. [Hoffmann, Rosenbaum and Yoshida 2013](notes/hoffmann-2013-lead-lag-non-synchronous-data.md), then [Nayyar 2026](notes/nayyar-2026-hyperliquid-lags-binance-lead-lag.md) and [Lim 2026](notes/lim-2026-binance-leads-wallets-anticipate.md): the estimator, then current Binance-Hyperliquid evidence.
8. [Young 2026](notes/young-2026-openmarket-polymarket-binance-dataset.md): the negative result on trading a small follower venue with ML.
9. [Shen et al. 2026](notes/shen-2026-misguided-price-discovery-overshooting.md): leadership flips in cascades.
10. [Kalshi 2026 filing](notes/kalshi-2026-nearperp-perpetual-contract-terms.md) and [CF Benchmarks 2026](notes/cfbenchmarks-2026-cme-cf-real-time-indices-methodology.md): what the target venue is anchored to.
11. [Plazuelo Pascual et al. 2025](notes/plazuelo-2025-price-discovery-cryptocurrency-markets.md) and [Augustin, Rubtsov and Shin 2023](notes/augustin-2023-impact-derivatives-spot-bitcoin-futures.md): CEX versus AMM, and how a new derivative changes integration.
