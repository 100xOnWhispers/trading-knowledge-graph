# Signals from options and CME futures: synthesis

Scope: what crypto options and regulated futures say about short-horizon direction and volatility, and whether any of it can be computed for SOL. Sixteen notes: nine read in full from papers, three from documentation or live exchange data, one practitioner white paper, and three from abstracts only. Realized-volatility forecasting belongs to topic 14 and is not repeated here.

A limit on this topic's coverage: web search was unavailable during the session, so sources were found from known identifiers, citation lists and catalogue listings. Several well-known works could not be opened and are listed at the end.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **SOL options are too thin to carry signals of their own.** A Deribit snapshot taken in this session shows SOL option open interest of about 76.5 million USD against 25.8 billion for BTC, 24-hour notional volume of 3.7 million against 1.15 billion, and near-the-money spreads about twice as wide ([deribit-2026-option-book-summary-sol-liquidity](notes/deribit-2026-option-book-summary-sol-liquidity.md)). Option-derived inputs for a SOL bot must come from BTC and ETH.

2. **Option flow carries volatility information first and directional information second, and the directional part sits in out-of-the-money contracts.** On Deribit, at-the-money flow behaves like volatility trading and directional demand is significant only in out-of-the-money buckets ([alexander-2023-net-buying-pressure-bitcoin-option-trades](notes/alexander-2023-net-buying-pressure-bitcoin-option-trades.md)). The equity classics agree on where the information is: deep out-of-the-money, high-leverage contracts ([pan-2004-information-option-volume-stock-prices](notes/pan-2004-information-option-volume-stock-prices.md)) and high-implied-volatility contracts and puts ([michael-2022-option-volume-imbalance-equity-returns](notes/michael-2022-option-volume-imbalance-equity-returns.md)).

3. **Publicly observable option flow is a weak, short-lived predictor.** Pan and Poteshman find that the public version of their signal predicts for one or two days and then reverses, while the non-public version carries the durable effect. Alexander and co-authors find that 40 to 46% of an hourly flow-driven implied-volatility move reverses in the next hour. Both point to price pressure and dealer inventory, not information, as the main content of what an outsider can see.

4. **Dealer hedging links options to the underlying, and its sign depends on dealer positioning.** Negative dealer gamma goes with trading in the direction of the move and positive gamma against it. For equities and other futures this is established by Baltussen and co-authors ([note in topic 11](../11-intraday-seasonality-tradfi/notes/baltussen-2021-hedging-demand-intraday-momentum.md)); for bitcoin the one peer-reviewed study states the same at abstract level ([atanasova-2025-aggregate-illiquidity-crypto-option-returns](notes/atanasova-2025-aggregate-illiquidity-crypto-option-returns.md)). Expiry pinning is real in a large futures market but small (at least 11 basis points) and can flip to anti-pinning when dealers are short ([golez-2012-pinning-sp500-futures](notes/golez-2012-pinning-sp500-futures.md)).

5. **Bitcoin implied variance exceeds realized variance on average, by a wide margin.** The one-month variance risk premium is about 0.14 in annualised variance units ([almeida-2024-risk-premia-bitcoin-market](notes/almeida-2024-risk-premia-bitcoin-market.md)); realized minus implied volatility was negative in every year the Deribit study tabulates ([alexander-2023](notes/alexander-2023-net-buying-pressure-bitcoin-option-trades.md)). Implied volatility is therefore a biased forecast, and it beats time-series models only beyond about a week ([hoang-2020-forecasting-bitcoin-volatility-options-market](notes/hoang-2020-forecasting-bitcoin-volatility-options-market.md), abstract only).

6. **The bitcoin smile is not the equity skew.** It is roughly symmetric, calls trade as much as puts, and upside tails carry a large share of the risk premium, most of all in calm markets (Alexander 2023, Almeida 2024, and the "action gauge" remark in [deribit-2021-dvol-implied-volatility-index](notes/deribit-2021-dvol-implied-volatility-index.md)). Rules imported from equity index options that read call demand as complacency or put skew as fear do not carry over unchanged.

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Sign of the variance premium as a return predictor.** In US equities a high premium precedes high quarterly returns ([bollerslev-2007-expected-stock-returns-variance-risk-premia](notes/bollerslev-2007-expected-stock-returns-variance-risk-premia.md)). In bitcoin the regime averages point the other way (Almeida 2024). Neither result is at an intraday horizon, and the bitcoin one rests on two cluster means.

- **How to compute dealer gamma.** The popular formula assumes dealers are long all calls and short all puts ([squeezemetrics-2017-gamma-exposure-gex](notes/squeezemetrics-2017-gamma-exposure-gex.md)); that assumption has no support in Deribit flow, where customers sell out-of-the-money options on both sides. The trade-signed alternative ([amberdata-2026-gamma-exposure-gex-documentation](notes/amberdata-2026-gamma-exposure-gex-documentation.md)) is more defensible but proprietary and unvalidated in public. No source read here reports the effect of bitcoin dealer gamma on spot or perpetual returns as a number.

- **Pinning and "max pain" in crypto.** No crypto study of expiry pinning was found. The best transferable evidence (Golez and Jackwerth) says pinning in S&P 500 futures came from customers reselling and exercising physically delivered American options; for cash-settled European options the same paper finds repulsion from the strike. Deribit options are cash-settled and European, so the popular claim has the weaker mechanism behind it.

- **Does option flow predict the underlying in crypto at all?** The Deribit paper tests implied-volatility changes, not returns. The equity papers test daily and overnight returns across hundreds of stocks with data fields (opening trades, participant class) that crypto venues do not publish. A direct test of signed BTC option flow against subsequent perpetual returns was not found.

- **CME basis and gaps.** The only CME-specific source opened shows that the CME basis differs from the carry implied by ETF options by about 2.6 points a year on average, with wide swings ([mallory-2026-implied-etf-carry-cme-bitcoin](notes/mallory-2026-implied-etf-carry-cme-bitcoin.md)); it is a short preprint with summary statistics only. No rigorous source on CME weekend gaps and their "fill" was found, and CME's own pages could not be reached to confirm current trading hours.

- **Machine learning.** The catalogue listings checked for 2020 to 2026 contain many papers on pricing, hedging and surface modelling of crypto options with neural networks and reinforcement learning, and very few that use option-derived features to predict the underlying. The two opened here are a surface-completion model ([singh-2026-hybrid-convolutional-vae-crypto-volatility-surfaces](notes/singh-2026-hybrid-convolutional-vae-crypto-volatility-surfaces.md)) and an option-strategy agent trained with reinforcement learning on equities ([xie-2026-stock-options-trading-agents-implied-distributions](notes/xie-2026-stock-options-trading-agents-implied-distributions.md)), both with short samples. Abstracts seen but not opened report a negative result for deep hedging on Deribit data against a classical no-trade band (Kumar, arXiv 2608.29025) and a language-model study of dealer gamma patterns in S&P 500 options whose detection accuracy stayed stable "even as economic profitability varies quarterly" (Regan and Xie, arXiv 2512.17923). No evolutionary-algorithm work on option-derived signals was found.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. Does hourly signed out-of-the-money BTC option flow (`D_C`) predict BTC and SOL perpetual returns over the next 5 minutes to 4 hours once perpetual order-flow imbalance and funding are controlled for? The papers predict a small effect that reverses.
2. Do the intraday momentum and reversal features from topic 13 change sign or strength with the sign of trade-signed BTC dealer gamma? This is the direct test of the abstract-level claim in Atanasova and co-authors, and it needs an inventory series built from the trade feed.
3. Around the daily, weekly and monthly 08:00 UTC Deribit expiries, does BTC drift toward or away from the largest-gamma strike during the 30-minute settlement window, and does volatility change after expiry when gamma rolls off? Condition on dealer gamma sign; expect effects of a few basis points at most.
4. Does the last hour's change in short-dated BTC or ETH implied volatility add to a SOL realized-volatility forecast that already contains the topic 14 model terms?
5. Is the variance premium regime (high or low implied variance cluster) useful for switching between trend and reversal rules on SOL, and with which sign?
6. How large is SOL option volume against SOL perpetual volume by hour, on Deribit and on other venues not measured here (CME options on Solana futures, Bybit, OKX, Binance)? If the ratio stays near the level found in the snapshot, drop SOL-specific option features.
7. Do CME bitcoin and Solana futures still close for weekends and a daily break, and if so, do reopening gaps relative to the continuous spot index have any predictive content for the following hours? Not answerable from the sources opened.
8. Does a surface anomaly score lead or lag large perpetual moves? The paper that proposes it reports coincidence only.

## Works that matter but were not opened

No note exists for these; details are from memory or from an abstract and should be checked before use.

- Barbon and Buraschi, "Gamma Fragility" (working paper, 2020); Ni, Pearson and Poteshman, "Stock price clustering on option expiration dates" (Journal of Financial Economics, 2005); Dim, Eraker and Vilkov, "0DTEs: Trading, Gamma Risk and Volatility Propagation" (working paper, 2024). All are hosted only on a site that refused automated access.
- Ni, Pearson, Poteshman and White, "Does Option Trading Have a Pervasive Impact on Underlying Stock Prices?" (Review of Financial Studies, 2021): the abstract reports a non-informational channel by which dealer hedge rebalancing affects stock return volatility and the probability of large moves.
- Soebhag, "Option gamma and stock returns" (Journal of Empirical Finance, 2023): the abstract reports that stocks with low net gamma exposure have higher subsequent realized volatility through a non-informational channel.
- Wei, Koutmos and Zhu, "Are Bitcoin option traders speculative or informed?" (Finance Research Letters, 2024): the abstract poses the question against investor-attention spikes but does not state the answer.
- Alexander, Chen, Deng and Wang, "Arbitrage opportunities and efficiency tests in crypto derivatives" (Journal of Financial Markets, 2024): the abstract reports that option and perpetual markets are becoming more efficient, with profitable parity violations remaining in high-volume or congested periods.
- Portnaya, "Do Prediction Markets Match Option Prices?" (arXiv 2606.19517): the abstract reports a mean gap of about 6 percentage points between prediction-market prices and option-implied binary values for bitcoin thresholds, with a half-life near four hours. Relevant to topic 06.
