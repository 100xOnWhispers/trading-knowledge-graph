# Machine learning on engineered features for crypto return prediction: synthesis

Scope: supervised learning for return or direction prediction at minutes to days from engineered bar, flow and derivatives features; labeling schemes; feature importance; adaptation to non-stationarity; ensembling; economic value after costs; and time-series foundation models where tested on financial or crypto data. Eighteen notes. Two are abstract-only (Cakici, Fischer), one source is thin on detail (G-Research), and several key works could not be opened at all; see the gaps at the end of "Where it disagrees or is weak".

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Predictability exists but is small.** On minute-to-hour bitcoin bars, six model classes reach 50.9% to 56.0% directional accuracy out of sample, rising with horizon ([Jaquart 2021](notes/jaquart-2021-short-term-bitcoin-prediction-machine-learning.md)). On daily data, individual models range from 46% to 60% and several are below 50% ([Sebastiao 2021](notes/sebastiao-2021-forecasting-trading-crypto-machine-learning.md)). In equities the best monthly out-of-sample R2 is 0.40% ([Gu 2020](notes/gu-2020-empirical-asset-pricing-machine-learning.md)), and a strong CatBoost benchmark on daily returns has a slightly negative R2 with about 51% accuracy ([Rahimikia 2025](notes/rahimikia-2025-revisiting-time-series-foundation-models-finance.md)). A backtest showing much more than this should be treated as a suspected leak.

2. **Shallow and simple beats deep and complex.** Neural networks peak at three layers and trees select fewer than six leaves in equities (Gu 2020). On bitcoin minute bars the tuned boosting depth was 1 (Jaquart 2021). Logistic regression matched or beat a neural network and a random forest overfit badly on 10 months of 15-minute bars ([Akyildirim 2021](notes/akyildirim-2021-prediction-cryptocurrency-returns-machine-learning.md)). In the crypto cross-section added complexity brings limited benefit ([Cakici 2024](notes/cakici-2024-machine-learning-cross-section-crypto-returns.md), abstract only). A one-layer linear model beat five transformers on every benchmark, and the naive forecast beat them all on exchange rates ([Zeng 2022](notes/zeng-2022-are-transformers-effective-time-series-forecasting.md)).

3. **Features matter more than model class, and the important ones are few and stable.** Recent own-returns dominate importance in every study that reports it (Gu 2020; Jaquart 2021; [Alessandretti 2018](notes/alessandretti-2018-anticipating-cryptocurrency-prices-machine-learning.md)); liquidity and volatility come next. The top three Kaggle finishers all used LightGBM and all credited feature engineering ([G-Research 2022](notes/gresearch-2022-crypto-forecasting-competition-wrap-up.md)). On Binance perps the same microstructure features hold the same SHAP rank and shape across five assets ([Bieganowski 2026](notes/bieganowski-2026-explainable-patterns-crypto-microstructure.md)).

4. **Costs decide the outcome at short horizons.** Every bitcoin strategy at 1 to 60 minutes turned negative at 30 bps round-trip (Jaquart 2021). Daily long-short equity signals with Sharpe above 5 gross are all negative at 40 bps (Rahimikia 2025). Where net profit is reported it comes from trading rarely (Sebastiao 2021), from a market-neutral panel with a tiny net margin of 7.1 bps a day ([Fischer 2019](notes/fischer-2019-statistical-arbitrage-cryptocurrency-random-forest.md), abstract only), or from longer bars (20 and 30 minutes better than 10 in [Slepaczuk 2024](notes/slepaczuk-2024-supervised-autoencoders-triple-barrier-crypto.md)).

5. **Forecast accuracy and trading profit are different objectives.** Higher accuracy did not mean higher strategy return (Jaquart 2021); negative R2 coexisted with strong ranking portfolios (Rahimikia 2025); models chosen on validation trading return had poor hit rates but usable ensembles (Sebastiao 2021). The 2026 review's principle is "train for the decision actually made" ([Zhu 2026](notes/zhu-2026-ai-equity-crypto-markets-profitability-limits.md)).

6. **Off-the-shelf foundation models do not forecast returns.** Zero-shot TimesFM is below chance on 4 of 7 horizons ([Fu 2024](notes/fu-2024-financial-fine-tuning-timesfm.md)); zero-shot Chronos has R2 of -47% to -1% on daily stock returns and fine-tuning does not fix it (Rahimikia 2025). Finance-native pretraining is what helps (Rahimikia 2025; [Shi 2025](notes/shi-2025-kronos-foundation-model-financial-klines.md)).

7. **Models must be refreshed.** Very short training windows won in a daily crypto panel (Alessandretti 2018); cheap incremental updates matched or beat full retraining in backtest return in seven of eight cases ([Zhao 2023](notes/zhao-2023-doubleadapt-incremental-learning-stock-trend.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Which model is best.** Akyildirim's abstract says SVM, its own Model Confidence Set says logistic regression and random forest. Jaquart finds GRU, LSTM and boosting roughly tied. The [Fang 2022](notes/fang-2022-cryptocurrency-trading-comprehensive-survey.md) survey lists studies with opposite LSTM-versus-others conclusions. The honest reading is that differences between competent models are inside the noise.
- **Whether sequence models add anything on bar data.** Jaquart's RNNs were marginally best at 1 and 60 minutes; Zeng and Rahimikia say linear models and boosted trees are the bar to clear; Kronos claims a large RankIC gain from finance-specific pretraining but shows no crypto trading result. No source tests a transformer against LightGBM on crypto perp features under costs.
- **Accuracy levels.** Akyildirim reports 55 to 65% (up to 69%) with no costs on a 10-month sample; Jaquart reports 51 to 56% on a cleaner design. The lower range is the better prior.
- **Whether profits are in tradable assets.** Cakici (abstract) says crypto ML alphas concentrate in small illiquid coins; Fischer reports a net profit on 40 coins in 2018 and immediately qualifies it with volume limits; Bieganowski finds the taker edge on BTC is not distinguishable from buy-and-hold while small-cap perps show large backtest returns.
- **Perp-specific features are almost untested.** No source in this folder uses funding, open interest or liquidations as inputs to a return classifier at minutes to hours. One search result (a Thai journal article, not opened) reportedly found ML funding-rate forecasts did not beat a static rule. Zhu 2026 supplies the perp P&L and leakage checklist but no empirical model.
- **Labeling evidence is thin.** Triple-barrier and meta-labeling are supported here by a student capstone on E-mini futures with no costs ([Singh 2019](notes/singh-2019-meta-labeling-triple-barrier-signal-efficacy.md)) and one crypto conference paper benchmarked only against buy-and-hold (Slepaczuk 2024). No controlled comparison of fixed-horizon versus triple-barrier labels on the same crypto features was found.
- **Evaluation quality.** Single train-test splits, bull-market test windows, comparison with buy-and-hold instead of simple rules, frictionless fills, and no multiple-testing control are common (Alessandretti 2018 is the extreme case). Methods for correcting this belong to topic 26.
- **Not accessed.** López de Prado's "Advances in Financial Machine Learning" (book; SSRN companion papers blocked), the Kaggle winners' own write-ups, Filippou, Rapach and Thimsen on crypto return predictability (SSRN), Jaquart, Koepke and Weinhardt (2022) on crypto ML trading, Gradojevic et al. on random forests with technical indicators (closed access), Kelly and Xiu's "Financial Machine Learning" survey, Avramov, Cheng and Metzker on ML profits and limits to arbitrage, and Tan et al. on LLM forecasters (known here only through Zhu 2026). The web-search budget for the session was exhausted for most of the work, so 2025 to 2026 coverage is partial.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. What directional accuracy and IC does a depth-1 to depth-3 LightGBM achieve on SOL at 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes using only Binance or Bybit bar features, and does it sit in Jaquart's 52 to 56% band? Does accuracy still rise with horizon?
2. How much do perp-native features (funding level and change, open-interest change, liquidation volume, perp-spot basis, cross-venue lead returns) add over lagged returns, measured by permutation importance and by net P&L? This is the largest gap in the literature.
3. At Kalshi's actual fee and spread, what is the break-even hit rate per horizon, and at which horizon does predicted edge first exceed it? Jaquart's negative result was at 30 bps; repeat at realistic maker and taker costs.
4. Fixed-horizon sign label versus triple-barrier label versus cross-sectional median-relative label, same features, same model, same walk-forward folds: which gives the best net result?
5. Meta-labeling on the bot's existing primary rules (trend, order-flow imbalance, funding extremes): does a classifier filter raise precision enough to pay for the trades it removes?
6. Supermajority voting across logistic regression, boosted trees and a small GRU as an intraday trade filter, with symmetric long and short.
7. Update policy: daily rolling refit, hourly warm-start incremental update, or short-window refit. Compare on the same forward period, including the 10 October 2025 crash.
8. Are feature importances and SHAP shapes for SOL stable month to month, and do they match BTC and ETH? A pooled multi-asset model versus a SOL-only model.
9. Does a Kronos forecast on SOL 5 to 60 minute bars, evaluated only after its pretraining cutoff, add anything to LightGBM as an extra feature? Does any sequence model pass Zeng's linear, naive and shuffle tests?
10. Do signals trained on Binance SOL perps transfer to Kalshi's SOL perp given its lower leverage, different participants and 8-hour funding, and with what lag?
11. How long a frozen-model paper-trading period is needed before a result is distinguishable from luck, given the leaderboard instability G-Research reported over three live months?
