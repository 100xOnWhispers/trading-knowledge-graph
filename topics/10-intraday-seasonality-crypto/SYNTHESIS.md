# Topic 10: Time-of-day and day-of-week patterns in crypto

Scope: intraday and intraweek periodicity in crypto returns, volatility, volume and liquidity, including funding timestamps, top-of-hour effects, US session and CME effects, options expiry, and changes after spot ETFs. Fifteen source notes. Coverage note: the shared web-search budget for this session ran out early, so later sources were found by following citation lists and known URLs; see "Where it disagrees or is weak" for what that left out.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Second moments are strongly periodic; this is the robust result.** Volume, volatility and illiquidity follow a repeatable daily curve with a trough in the early UTC morning (about 01:00 to 05:00) and a peak in the UTC afternoon (about 14:00 to 17:00), the European afternoon and US morning. It holds on Bitstamp in 2014 to 2017 ([Eross et al.](notes/eross-2019-intraday-dynamics-bitcoin.md)), on Binance, Coinbase and Uniswap in 2019 to 2021 ([Hansen et al.](notes/hansen-2021-periodicity-crypto-volatility-liquidity.md)), and across 1,940 pairs on 38 exchanges ([Brauneis et al.](notes/brauneis-2025-crypto-world-trades-tea-time.md)). Abstract-level reads of Wang, Liu and Hsu (2020, Finance Research Letters) and Dyhrberg, Foley and Svec (2018, Economics Letters) say the same; neither has a note.
2. **The pattern is global, not local, and altcoins share it.** Exchange location shifts the volume curve a little, but the afternoon-UTC peak appears on every continent and in pairs with and without BTC ([Brauneis et al.](notes/brauneis-2025-crypto-world-trades-tea-time.md); eleven coins in [Petukhina et al.](notes/petukhina-2021-rise-machines-intraday-crypto.md)). This is the best available basis for assuming SOL follows the same clock.
3. **Weekends are quieter.** Volume and volatility fall on Saturday and Sunday in every study that looks ([Hansen et al.](notes/hansen-2021-periodicity-crypto-volatility-liquidity.md), [Petukhina et al.](notes/petukhina-2021-rise-machines-intraday-crypto.md), [Kinateder and Papavassiliou](notes/kinateder-2021-calendar-effects-bitcoin-returns-volatility.md), [Baur et al.](notes/baur-2019-bitcoin-time-of-day-day-of-week.md)). CME's own figures put weekend volatility at about 75% of weekday volatility for 2020 to 2026 ([CME Group](notes/cmegroup-2026-aligning-crypto-derivatives-247-trading.md)). Volatility is highest early in the week.
4. **Clock-time and funding-time bursts are real.** Volatility and volume jump in the first minute of each hour, after minutes 15, 30 and 45, and every five minutes, most strongly at funding hours ([Hansen et al.](notes/hansen-2021-periodicity-crypto-volatility-liquidity.md)). Around each 8-hour funding settlement, spot and perpetual volume rise by about 50%, spreads widen by about 3% and order-flow toxicity rises by about 17% ([funding-cycle paper](notes/anonymous-2025-perpetual-futures-funding-cycle-market-quality.md)).
5. **Mean-return seasonality is weak and unstable.** Large multi-exchange and GARCH-based studies find no persistent hour-of-day or day-of-week return effect ([Baur et al.](notes/baur-2019-bitcoin-time-of-day-day-of-week.md), [Kinateder and Papavassiliou](notes/kinateder-2021-calendar-effects-bitcoin-returns-volatility.md)). Where return effects are found they are small, conditional, or in-sample.
6. **Institutional conventions move the clock.** After US spot ETFs, BTC volume concentrated in the 3pm to 4pm New York benchmark window and the weekend share fell to 16% from a 28% peak ([Kaiko](notes/kaiko-2024-btc-etfs-impact-spot-market-structure.md)). Deribit options activity clusters at the 08:00 UTC expiry and at the NYSE open ([Hoang and Phan](notes/hoang-2026-time-of-day-bitcoin-options.md)). CME moved to 24/7 trading on 29 May 2026 ([CME Group](notes/cmegroup-2026-aligning-crypto-derivatives-247-trading.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Monday effect.** [Caporale and Plastun](notes/caporale-2017-day-of-week-crypto.md) find higher Bitcoin returns on Mondays in 2013 to 2017. [Kinateder and Papavassiliou](notes/kinateder-2021-calendar-effects-bitcoin-returns-volatility.md) find no significant Monday return once volatility is modelled (only higher Monday volatility). A neural-network ablation finds weekday information useful for BTC but not ETH or ADA, and contradicts itself on which day is best ([Tosunoglu et al.](notes/tosunoglu-2023-neural-network-day-of-week-crypto.md)). Treat weekday return effects as unproven.
- **The 21:00 to 23:00 UTC return window.** The Quantpedia studies ([2022](notes/padysak-2022-seasonal-intraday-overnight-anomalies-bitcoin.md), [2023](notes/quantpedia-2023-seasonality-of-bitcoin.md)) report 33% to 41% annualised from two hours a day, with no costs, in-sample, long-only in a rising asset, and with an admitted drawdown in 2022 to 2023. [Brauneis et al.](notes/brauneis-2025-crypto-world-trades-tea-time.md) independently see positive returns late in the UTC day, but give no strategy test. Academic null results (item 5 above) point the other way.
- **Humans or machines.** [Petukhina et al.](notes/petukhina-2021-rise-machines-intraday-crypto.md) read the daily cycle as proof that humans dominate; [Hansen et al.](notes/hansen-2021-periodicity-crypto-volatility-liquidity.md) show machine-like bursts at round clock times that strengthened from 2019 to 2021. Both can hold, at different time scales.
- **Liquidity at the peak.** Dyhrberg et al. (abstract) report the lowest spreads during US hours on US exchanges in 2018; [Brauneis et al.](notes/brauneis-2025-crypto-world-trades-tea-time.md) find illiquidity highest at the 16:00 to 17:00 UTC peak using a high-low spread estimator. The measures differ (quoted spread versus a volatility-contaminated estimate), so the cost of trading at the peak is unresolved.
- **Intraday momentum.** [Shen et al.](notes/shen-2022-bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum.md) find first-half-hour to last-half-hour momentum with out-of-sample support but breakeven costs of 3 to 10 bps. Wen, Bouri, Xu and Zhao (2022, abstract only, no note) report both momentum and reversal in BTC, ETH, LTC and XRP.
- **Thin or missing areas.**
  - No study of Solana's intraday seasonality was found. Altcoin evidence is pooled, not SOL-specific.
  - Almost nothing peer-reviewed uses post-2023 data. ETF-era evidence is one vendor blog and one exchange article.
  - No rigorous test of the CME gap-fill claim was found; the cited fill rates are unsourced.
  - No source measures return direction around funding timestamps or options expiry; the evidence is on activity and toxicity only.
  - No end-of-month or turn-of-month study at intraday resolution was found.
  - Machine learning: the sources that use learning methods apply them to the seasonal baseline (splines in Petukhina et al., periodic GARCH in Hansen et al., a mixture network and boosted trees for volume in [Antulov-Fantulin et al.](notes/antulov-fantulin-2020-temporal-mixture-intraday-volume.md), a small neural net in Tosunoglu et al.). No reinforcement-learning or evolutionary-search study that targets clock effects in crypto was located in this session. That is a gap in this search, not evidence that none exists.
  - Kaiko and Amberdata hourly liquidity reports beyond the one Kaiko post were not reached.

Practical reading of the table: clock features have strong support as inputs to volatility, volume, cost and risk models, and weak support as direct return predictors. For a learning-based bot (supervised, reinforcement or evolutionary), that argues for feeding the agent deseasonalised state variables plus the clock, and for requiring every clock-based return feature to pass an out-of-sample ablation. A search procedure given 24 hours times 7 days times several regimes will always find some profitable cell in-sample.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. Does SOL's volatility and volume curve peak at the same UTC hour as BTC's, and did the peak move toward the US close after spot ETFs (BTC in 2024, SOL later)?
2. At which funding timestamps does SOL activity burst: offshore 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC, Hyperliquid's hourly funding, or Kalshi's own 8-hour schedule? Is there a signed drift against the paying side in the hour before settlement, and does it scale with the funding rate?
3. Is the minute-of-hour burst present on SOL perps, and is the first minute after a bar close followed by continuation or reversal over the next 5 to 15 minutes?
4. Does the 21:00 to 23:00 UTC return window exist in SOL after costs and funding, out of sample from 2023 onward, and only in uptrend or high-volatility states?
5. Does first-half-hour to last-half-hour momentum around the US equity session hold for SOL, and is the reversal from the second-to-last half hour still there?
6. How did the Sunday-evening reopen behaviour (Monday first-hour volatility, weekend lead-lag between offshore perps and regulated venues) change after CME went 24/7 on 29 May 2026?
7. Are Kalshi SOL perp spreads and depth worst at the global volatility peak (as the high-low estimator suggests) or best (as 2018 US quoted spreads suggest)? This decides whether to trade or avoid the peak.
8. Do large Deribit expiry days (Friday 08:00 UTC) change SOL return or volatility in the hours before and after, once the coincident funding timestamp is controlled for?
9. Does deseasonalising inputs (volume, volatility, order-flow imbalance) improve a SOL direction model more than adding raw clock features does?
10. How fast do the seasonal curves drift? Compare rolling 30-, 90- and 365-day estimates to choose a re-estimation window.
