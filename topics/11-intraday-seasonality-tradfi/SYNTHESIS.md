# Topic 11: Intraday and calendar patterns in futures, FX and equities

Scope: the non-crypto literature on time-of-day, session, fixing, calendar and pre-announcement patterns, and what transfers to a SOL perpetual traded at minutes to hours. Crypto-specific seasonality is topic 10. Eighteen notes: seventeen read as full text (two of them central-bank blog updates read as rendered text, one working paper read in part) and one abstract-only (Gao et al.).

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Volatility and volume seasonality is real, large and stable; mean-return seasonality is small and fragile.** Andersen and Bollerslev find a five-fold range in average absolute 5-minute FX returns over the day and no systematic pattern in mean returns in the same data ([andersen-1997](notes/andersen-1997-intraday-periodicity-volatility-persistence.md)). Every later paper that finds a mean pattern reports it in basis points per day. The practical order of priority is therefore: deseasonalize volatility and volume first, treat directional clock effects as a weak second-order input.

2. **Where directional patterns exist, they come from predictable, price-insensitive flow meeting limited intermediary capacity.** The explanations converge across asset classes:
   - scheduled institutional execution repeating at the same clock time ([heston-2010](notes/heston-2010-intraday-patterns-cross-section-returns.md));
   - gamma hedging and leveraged-product rebalancing into the close ([baltussen-2021](notes/baltussen-2021-hedging-demand-intraday-momentum.md));
   - end-of-session order imbalance absorbed by dealers and released when the next region arrives ([boyarchenko-2023](notes/boyarchenko-2023-overnight-drift.md));
   - benchmark-fix demand pre-hedged by dealers ([krohn-2024](notes/krohn-2024-fx-fixings-returns-around-clock.md));
   - regional participants buying foreign currency in their own hours ([breedon-2013](notes/breedon-2013-intraday-patterns-fx-returns-order-flow.md));
   - month-end cash needs interacting with settlement lags ([etula-2020](notes/etula-2020-dash-for-cash-month-end-liquidity.md)).
   In each case the pattern is a drift into the liquidity event followed by a reversal, and it is stronger when volatility is high and intermediaries are constrained.

3. **Returns should be decomposed by session, not treated as one close-to-close number.** Overnight and intraday components behave like different assets: each persists in its own window and reverses in the other ([lou-2019](notes/lou-2019-tug-of-war-overnight-intraday.md)); different premia are paid at different hours ([bogousslavsky-2021](notes/bogousslavsky-2021-cross-section-intraday-overnight-returns.md)); around-the-clock futures and FX returns concentrate in specific windows ([boyarchenko-2023](notes/boyarchenko-2023-overnight-drift.md), [krohn-2024](notes/krohn-2024-fx-fixings-returns-around-clock.md)).

4. **Gross effects rarely survive a price taker's costs.** The authors themselves report this: Heston et al. (spread wipes out the periodicity trade), Boyarchenko et al. (Sharpe 1.1 becomes -0.5 after spreads), Krohn et al. (most profits disappear), Breedon and Ranaldo (profitable only in EUR/USD), Neely and Weller (nothing left after costs and liquid-hours restriction), Baltussen et al. (costs not modelled; positive only at a one-tick cost in S&P futures). The realistic uses are trade timing, conditioning of other signals and risk scaling.

5. **Conditioning variables carry the information, not the clock.** Closing signed volume and VIX (overnight drift), dealer gamma (intraday momentum), VIX (fix reversals), prior selling pressure (month-end), event importance (pre-FOMC).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Persistence after publication.** The two best-known clock anomalies have both decayed in their authors' own follow-ups. The overnight drift is flat in 2021 to 2025 because closing-imbalance dispersion collapsed ([boyarchenko-2026](notes/boyarchenko-2026-disappearing-overnight-drift.md)). The pre-FOMC drift ([lucca-2015](notes/lucca-2015-pre-fomc-announcement-drift.md)) survived only before press-conference meetings in 2011 to 2018 and, per an abstract, largely disappeared after 2015 ([lucca-2018](notes/lucca-2018-pre-fomc-drift-recent-evidence.md)). Neither source covers 2020 to 2026 for FOMC.
- **Data mining.** Sullivan, Timmermann and White show the best of 9,452 daily calendar rules has a nominal p-value below 0.002 and a search-adjusted p-value of 0.24 ([sullivan-2001](notes/sullivan-2001-dangers-data-mining-calendar-effects.md)). Day-of-week effects have no surviving mechanism in this set of notes. Turn-of-month does have one (Etula et al.), which is the difference between the two.
- **Which predictor drives end-of-day momentum.** Gao et al. use the first half-hour (including the overnight gap) ([gao-2018](notes/gao-2018-market-intraday-momentum.md), abstract only); Baltussen et al. find the whole rest-of-day return works better across 60+ futures and attribute it to hedging, not late-informed trading.
- **Sign of the end-of-day effect.** At index level the last half-hour continues the day's move; in the cross-section of single stocks it reverses it ([baltussen-2026](notes/baltussen-2026-end-of-day-reversal.md)). A single altcoin perp sits between an index and a single name, so the sign for SOL is an open question.
- **Cross-section versus time series.** Heston, Lou-Polk-Skouras, Bogousslavsky and the 2026 reversal paper are cross-sectional results on thousands of stocks. They do not imply the same pattern in one instrument's own time series.
- **Transfer to a market with no close.** Most equity results depend on a hard close, an opening auction or settlement conventions. The 24-hour evidence (FX and E-mini) is thinner: two FX papers, one futures paper and its authors' own report of decay.
- **Machine learning and evolutionary evidence is mixed and cost-bound.** Genetic programming on half-hourly FX with time of day as an input found only untradable negative autocorrelation, no better than an autoregression ([neely-2003](notes/neely-2003-intraday-technical-trading-fx-genetic.md)). LASSO on a wide cross-section of lagged factor returns gives small but real out-of-sample predictability and a cost-adjusted Sharpe of 0.79 on SPY, where costs are a fraction of a basis point ([aleti-2025](notes/aleti-2025-intraday-market-return-predictability-factor-zoo.md)). A PPO agent with time-left and position features beats simple benchmarks on futures but stops being profitable above about 0.16 bp commission ([goluza-2024](notes/goluza-2024-deep-rl-intraday-trading-positional-context.md)). None of these treats seasonality as the primary object; time of day is one feature among many, and the reinforcement-learning evidence is grade C.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. **Periodic volatility factor.** What is the 24-hour, 7-day volatility and volume shape for SOL on the large venues, how fast does it drift, and does it depend on the volatility level (as in equities) or not (as in FX)? How do US daylight-saving shifts move it?
2. **Liquidity-cliff reversal.** Does aggressor-signed volume over the last US hour (aggregated across Binance, Bybit, OKX, Hyperliquid) predict SOL returns over Asian hours with a negative sign, asymmetrically after sell-offs and more strongly when implied volatility is high? Is the dispersion of that imbalance stable?
3. **Session decomposition.** Split SOL returns into Asia, Europe and US windows. Is there same-session persistence and cross-session reversal over trailing weeks? Does SOL's trend return accrue in a particular session?
4. **Forced-flow windows.** Is there move-so-far momentum into the US equity close (leveraged crypto ETF rebalancing), the Deribit 08:00 UTC expiry window, CME reference-rate windows, or funding timestamps? Does the sign flip with estimated dealer gamma? Is it followed by reversal?
5. **Index versus single-name sign.** Into those windows, does the market-wide crypto move continue while SOL-minus-BTC reverses, as the equity evidence would suggest?
6. **Fixing V-shape.** Around crypto benchmark windows used for futures settlement and ETF pricing, is there a pre-window drift and post-window reversal in SOL or BTC?
7. **Same-clock continuation.** Does SOL's return in a given 30-minute bucket predict the same bucket on following days after removing the market move, and is it large enough to help entry timing?
8. **Pre-event behaviour.** Do SOL volume and realized volatility fall before FOMC and CPI and is there any drift, in the current regime only? With eight FOMC events a year, how many years are needed for a t-statistic of 2?
9. **Search-adjusted significance.** After a stationary-bootstrap Reality Check over the full hour-of-week grid and all session definitions tried, does any SOL clock effect in mean returns survive?
10. **Learning-based approaches under Kalshi costs.** With the venue's real fee and spread in the objective: does a sparse cross-asset lead-lag model (other perps, sector baskets, equity futures as predictors) clear costs at 15 to 60 minutes? Does adding cyclic time-of-day, time-to-funding and position features to an RL or supervised model add net value over the same model without them? Does an evolutionary search find anything beyond what a regularized linear model finds, and what is the break-even cost per trade?
