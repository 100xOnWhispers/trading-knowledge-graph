# Trade flow, informed trading and flow toxicity: synthesis

Scope: signals from executed trades (signed volume, taker imbalance, cumulative volume delta, sign autocorrelation, VPIN and other toxicity measures, Kyle lambda, trade classification), including machine-learning, reinforcement-learning and evolutionary methods applied to them. Resting order book imbalance is topic 07 and impact laws are topic 09. Eighteen notes; sixteen read in full text, two from abstracts.

## What the literature agrees on

1. **Sign trades before aggregating, and use the exchange's aggressor flag.** With true signs available, bar-level classification throws information away. In E-mini futures the transaction-level tick rule misclassified 11.6%, the tick rule on 60-second bars 38.7%, and the best bulk-volume variant 23.2% ([andersen-2013](notes/andersen-2013-order-flow-toxicity-perfect-classification.md)). On Bitstamp the tick rule was right only 76.87% of the time ([ma-2021](notes/ma-2021-accuracy-tick-rule-bitcoin.md), abstract only). Recent crypto work uses the Binance taker flag directly ([kim-2026](notes/kim-2026-quarter-hour-effect-crypto-futures.md), [kitron-2026](notes/kitron-2026-short-horizon-mean-reversion-crypto.md)).

2. **Signed flow is highly persistent, and that persistence is already priced.** Order signs have long memory (Hurst about 0.7) in equities ([lillo-2004](notes/lillo-2004-long-memory-efficient-market.md)), yet returns stay near-unpredictable because liquidity adjusts against expected flow and single-trade impact decays ([bouchaud-2004](notes/bouchaud-2004-fluctuations-response-financial-markets.md)). A high taker-buy ratio mostly forecasts more taker buying, not higher prices. The theory behind order splitting is [kyle-1985](notes/kyle-1985-continuous-auctions-insider-trading.md).

3. **Raw contemporaneous taker imbalance has little directional content at the bot's horizon; conditioning is what creates it.** At 15 minutes, lagged taker imbalance added nothing out of sample beyond the price path, but it identified when reversal applies: AUC 0.540 after flow-driven bars against 0.519 after flow-opposed bars ([kitron-2026](notes/kitron-2026-short-horizon-mean-reversion-crypto.md)). Imbalance at ordinary times had little predictive association with returns, while imbalance in the first 10 seconds of a quarter-hour predicted returns 4 to 12 hours ahead ([kim-2026](notes/kim-2026-quarter-hour-effect-crypto-futures.md)).

4. **Cross-venue flow leads.** Taker flow on leading venues predicts returns on lagging venues at sub-second horizons, and fee levels help determine who lags ([albers-2021](notes/albers-2021-fragmentation-cross-impact-bitcoin.md)). BTC and ETH toxicity measures help predict other coins' volatility-type statistics ([easley-2024](notes/easley-2024-microstructure-dynamics-crypto-markets.md)).

5. **Gross edges are a small fraction of fees.** About 0.5 bp per quarter-hour boundary against a 5 bp taker fee ([kim-2026](notes/kim-2026-quarter-hour-effect-crypto-futures.md)); 1.3 bp at best against a 5 bp round trip ([kitron-2026](notes/kitron-2026-short-horizon-mean-reversion-crypto.md)); every pairwise taker strategy negative at default fees ([albers-2021](notes/albers-2021-fragmentation-cross-impact-bitcoin.md)).

6. **Toxicity measures are about risk, not direction.** VPIN, Kyle lambda and the Roll measure were tested against volatility, spread, kurtosis and similar targets ([easley-2012](notes/easley-2012-flow-toxicity-liquidity-vpin.md), [easley-2021](notes/easley-2021-microstructure-machine-age.md), [easley-2024](notes/easley-2024-microstructure-dynamics-crypto-markets.md)). None of these papers claims they predict the sign of returns.

7. **One-sided flow punishes resting orders.** In the October 2025 crash a taker strategy profited while a maker strategy on the same signal was repeatedly filled on its bids ([bieganowski-2026](notes/bieganowski-2026-explainable-patterns-crypto-microstructure.md)); every reinforcement-learning market maker lost 5% to 10% in one 200-second sell-off ([sadighian-2020](notes/sadighian-2020-extending-drl-crypto-market-making.md)).

## Where it disagrees or is weak

- **Is VPIN more than a volatility proxy?** The VPIN authors report a 0.40 correlation with next-bucket absolute returns and top out-of-sample feature importance across 87 futures and five coins. Andersen and Bondarenko find the predictive content is a mechanical link to trading intensity, that VPIN from true signs is negatively related to future volatility, and that nothing remains after controlling for volume and realized volatility ([andersen-2014](notes/andersen-2014-vpin-flash-crash.md), [andersen-2013](notes/andersen-2013-order-flow-toxicity-perfect-classification.md)). The crypto VPIN evidence uses bulk classification on one-minute candles and no lagged-volatility benchmark, so it does not settle the question. The VPIN authors hold a financial interest in the measure.
- **Follow flow or fade it?** Reversal after flow-driven 15-minute bars, continuation over 4 to 12 hours after quarter-hour boundary flow, with a negative slope in the first half hour. These are consistent with each other only if horizon and measurement window are kept distinct; no paper tests both in one framework.
- **Does flow add value beyond the order book?** For liquidity-state prediction, flow added a small gain for ETH and none established for BTC once a book-state model was in place ([jeon-2026](notes/jeon-2026-order-flow-liquidity-state-crypto-futures.md)). Albers et al. argue the opposite on priors, that aggressive flow is the informative component.
- **Machine learning.** Tree ensembles are the workhorse and feature rankings are stable across assets ([bieganowski-2026](notes/bieganowski-2026-explainable-patterns-crypto-microstructure.md), [easley-2021](notes/easley-2021-microstructure-machine-age.md)). But at 15 minutes, boosted trees and an MLP lost to a three-parameter constrained logit out of sample ([kitron-2026](notes/kitron-2026-short-horizon-mean-reversion-crypto.md)). Online Bayesian updating of a small network beat static models for per-trade toxicity in FX ([cartea-2023](notes/cartea-2023-detecting-toxic-flow.md)), with client identity as the key feature, which crypto feeds lack.
- **Reinforcement learning.** No source shows that an RL agent extracts directional value from trade-flow features. The one RL paper with trade flow imbalance in the state never isolated its contribution, and only 38 of 168 experiments were profitable ([sadighian-2020](notes/sadighian-2020-extending-drl-crypto-market-making.md)). A November 2026 multi-agent RL paper on Binance perpetuals (M3FT, arXiv 2611.10586, opened but not noted) includes taker-buy activity among daily state features; its contribution is not isolated in the text read.
- **Evolutionary methods.** The evidence is thinnest here. One peer-reviewed study uses genetic programming to simulate markets, not to find tradable rules ([manahov-2021](notes/manahov-2021-efficiency-bitcoin-genetic-programming.md)); one short preprint tunes an RSI strategy with a genetic algorithm over eight months without costs or a tuning-method baseline ([tian-2025](notes/tian-2025-genetic-algorithm-crypto-strategy-optimization.md)). Neither uses signed flow.
- **Sample weakness.** Classic sign-memory results are equities from 1999 to 2002. Cross-venue lead-lag rests on eight days in 2021. SOL appears in only three samples and is the weakest of six assets in the quarter-hour study. Nothing covers Kalshi or Hyperliquid.
- **Sources seen but not noted.**
  - Scaillet, Treccani and Trevisan, "High-frequency jump analysis of the bitcoin market" (2017 working paper, University of Geneva archive): the abstract states that order flow imbalance, the preponderance of aggressive traders and a widening spread predict jumps on Mt. Gox, June 2011 to November 2013. Not noted because the venue and period are remote from current perps.
  - Cianini, "Order-flow toxicity and microstructure of crypto order books" (University of Padova master's thesis, 2022-2023): applies VPIN and Hawkes intensities to Binance Bitcoin data; its return classifiers reach 46.29% (OLS) and 52.77% (neural network) accuracy. Too weak for a note.
  - Silantyev, "Order flow analysis of cryptocurrency markets" (Digital Finance, 2019) and the Wang et al. Bitcoin VPIN and jumps paper were identified but could not be opened in this session, so the direct trade-flow-imbalance regression on a BitMEX perpetual and a Bitcoin-specific VPIN study are missing from this topic.
  - Neighbors: order flow imbalance from the book (Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov) is topic 07; impact laws are topic 09.

## Open questions worth testing on SOL perps

1. Does quarter-hour boundary imbalance on Binance SOL perp still predict 4 to 12 hour returns in 2025 and 2026, and does pooling BTC and ETH boundary flow strengthen the weak SOL result?
2. How long does Kalshi's SOL perp lag signed taker flow on Binance, Bybit, OKX and Hyperliquid? Is any of the lag reachable at 10 seconds to several minutes after Kalshi fees?
3. Does VPIN from true taker signs add anything to forecasts of SOL realized volatility or liquidation cascades beyond trailing realized volatility, trade count and implied volatility? Does bulk-classified VPIN merely track volatility, as the E-mini critique predicts?
4. Does flow surprise (signed flow minus its forecast from lagged signs) predict SOL returns at 1 to 60 minutes better than raw cumulative volume delta? What are the sign-memory and impact-decay exponents on SOL?
5. Does the flow-driven reversal at 15 minutes appear on Kalshi when the conditioning flow is measured on Binance, and is it capturable with passive entries given the adverse-selection evidence?
6. Does flow imbalance matter more for SOL when the book is stressed, and does it pass a flow-shuffle placebo once a book-state baseline is in place?
7. Does the Kalshi trade feed publish the taker side? If not, how accurate is the tick rule on its prints?
8. For any learned model: does a small constrained specification beat boosted trees and neural networks out of sample at 15 minutes to 4 hours, as it did on Binance candles?
9. For RL and genetic search: with fees, funding and realistic fills in the simulator, does either beat a supervised flow-feature model with a fixed threshold rule, and does genetic search beat random or Bayesian search at equal budget?
10. Do liquidation-driven prints, which are forced and not informed, need to be separated from discretionary taker flow before computing any of the above?
